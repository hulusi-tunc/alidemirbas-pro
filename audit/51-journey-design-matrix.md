# 69-journey design matrix — Phase 13, extended at Phase 27

One row per public journey, all **69**, grouped under the nine category headings
`audit/public-journey-scope.md` established. Those nine are a reading order, not the taxonomy:
the public library is **69 journeys in 18 categories**, and the two numbers are not versions of
each other. Several categories share a heading here — `retention` and `consent` are both under
heading 3, `subscription` and `scheduling` under heading 8, `risk`, `document`, `rollout` and
`incident` under heading 9 — so nine headings, eighteen categories and sixty-nine rows are three
different counts of three different things. The id list this matrix follows is
`PUBLIC_LIBRARY_IDS` in `src/lib/public-corpus.ts`, which is the 69 and which the build asserts
against the derived library at module load. (The scope file itself still states the partition at
its 52/21/73 state, before the seventeen additions of Phases 25–33; its **headings** are what this
matrix uses, not its counts.)

**RET-24 Churn Risk Escalation is in this matrix**, in section 3. The note this file used to carry
— that RET-24 had been removed from the public set by explicit decision — is corrected rather than
deleted, because the reversal is the more useful record. The removal rested on the claim that
giving RET-24 a customer message would duplicate RET-30's ownership of the same retention episode.
Its own `contact.competition` already arbitrates exactly that: exclusion group
`retention-outreach`, scope `account`, ranked *above generic retention intervention*, with
`onLoss: "suppressed"`. The hazard was already solved in its authored data, and the two reviews
that argued for removal had read its handoff nodes and not that block. It stays public with **one
recorded exception** to the channel rule — a public journey must carry at least one customer-facing
communication action, and RET-24 carries none — named with its reason in
`scripts/validate-public-scope.mjs` and printed as a `WARN` on every run, so it cannot pass
unnoticed and a second journey cannot join it without an edit there and a written reason. The full
account is `audit/public-scope-validation.md`.

**Seventeen of these rows are the additions of Phases 25–33**, in four batches: **A** ACQ-289,
RET-290, FUL-291, RET-292, RET-293, RET-294 · **B** RET-295, SUB-296, SUB-297, SUB-298, SUB-299 ·
**C** CON-300, FUL-301, FIN-302 · **D** SCH-303, SCH-304, REM-305. 51 + RET-24 + 17 = 69.

**Sources.** For the 52 rows that predate Phase 25 — the original 51 and RET-24, both of which
`audit/journey-blueprints.md` and `audit/channel-orchestration-map.md` cover — Trigger / Goal /
Key split / Stop / Handoff are from the blueprints, Model and Channels from the orchestration map,
ownership-transfer events from `audit/collision-review.md`, and every Timing value and wait key is
read back from `production/canonical-dump.json`. The seventeen additions have no blueprint and no
orchestration-map row: **every cell in them is read directly from the dump** — `entry` and the
trigger node's own `evidence` for Trigger, `objective` for Goal, the `execution: "communication"`
nodes reachable on one path for Touches, `orchestration.touches[]` with their `mandatory` flags
and `contact.localCap` for the justification, `channelStrategy.roles` filtered to the five customer
channels for Channels, each wait's `timeout.after` `Config` for Timing, the pivotal `condition`
node for Key split, `until[]` and the exit classes for Stop, and the `handoff` nodes for Handoff.
`audit/batch-b-notes.md`, `audit/batch-c-notes.md` and `audit/batch-d-notes.md` were used as a
cross-check on the touch counts and the `localCap` reasoning rather than as a source; where a batch
note and the dump could have disagreed the dump governs. On the eleven rows those three notes cover
they agree with the dump, once batch D's own late correction is applied — a `localCap` counts only
what a journey may choose *not* to send, so a `mandatory: true` message does not spend discretionary
budget, which is why SCH-303 reads 3 touches against a discretionary budget of 1 and REM-305 reads
2 against a budget of 1. **Batch A has no notes file**, so its six rows (ACQ-289, RET-290, FUL-291,
RET-292, RET-293, RET-294) rest on the dump alone and on nothing else.
`audit/new-journey-id-map.md` is the source for which category each addition sits in.

**How to read the cells.**

- **Touches** — the maximum customer-facing messages on *one path, to one recipient*. Not the
  node count: mutually exclusive branch messages are not added together. Where a journey reaches
  4+, the Model cell carries the justification.
- **Model** — Single · Sequential · Conditional · Parallel · Fallback · Segment-based ·
  Event-or-timeout. Nothing else is legal. A human or internal work item is **never** a model; it
  appears only in Handoff.
- **Channels** — Email, SMS, Push, WhatsApp, In-app only. `task` and `sales` are operational
  behaviour, not channels; where a journey raises an internal work item it is in Handoff.
  Four of the 69 declare `task` in canonical `channels` — ACT-13, FBK-43 and FBK-49 alongside
  `email` and `in-app`, and RET-24 as its only channel. All four are correct and cannot be
  deleted: `validate:canonical` requires a declared channel to be backed by an action that does
  the work. `publicChannels()` filters it at the publishing boundary, so it is absent from their
  Channels cells here — which leaves RET-24's cell empty of channels, the fact its recorded
  exception exists for.
- **Timing** — canonical `Config` values. A wait whose `after` is `required: true` with no default
  is written **"configured, no default"**; no number is invented for it. Ranges marked
  *(example-only)* carry `basis: "example-only"` in the dump and are illustrative defaults, not
  recommendations. **Not one of the seventeen additions carries an example-only timing**: every
  wait in all seventeen is `required: true` with no default, so every interval in their Timing
  cells reads "configured, no default" and no number appears in any of them. The only defaults
  they carry are touch budgets and cooldowns whose basis is `corpus-rule`.
- **Stop condition** — the event that ends the journey immediately, usually the goal being met.
- **Handoff** — who takes ownership and on what event. Journeys marked *(not public)* are real
  canonical targets outside the 69 — either in the 21 the product decision excludes or in the
  archived Operational Workflows surface; a handoff into one renders as a name, never a link.

  fallback (`next-eligible-role` or `same-role-other-channel`), but that is a *role-ordering* rule
  the router applies when picking a route, and none of their graphs re-sends a message because a
  route failed. Channel selection is not orchestration, which is the orchestration map's own rule.
  This is the check the brief asked for, and it still passes.
