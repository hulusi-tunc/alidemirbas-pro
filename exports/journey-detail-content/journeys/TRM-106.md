## TRM-106 — Account Closure

- Canonical ID: `TRM-106`
- Slug: `account-closure-request`
- Display name: Account Closure
- Canonical name (chain form): Account closure request → validate → resolve blockers → close
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/account-closure-request`
- Category: Entity merge, account closure, data lifecycle & terminal states
- Goal: cancellation-termination
- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: End an account relationship once the obligations that legitimately block it are resolved, and end nothing else.

> End an account relationship once the obligations that legitimately block it are resolved, and end nothing else.

### Trigger
- Event: `account_closure_requested`
- Meaning: a closure request against an identified account
- Not enough on its own: a cancelled subscription, which ends a commercial relationship rather than the account; a data deletion request, which is a different lifecycle with a different authority; a long period of inactivity
- Requires: a closure request against an identified account
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the account and this closure request
- instance: account_id + closure_request_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a closure request against an identified account
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the account and this closure request
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Account closure is not data deletion.
- [canonical rule] Account closure is not subscription cancellation unless a contract explicitly couples them.
- [canonical rule] Closure does not silently erase audit or history.
- [canonical rule] A blocker is named specifically. An unexplained refusal is not a blocker, it is a support case.

### Recommended orchestration · notice-then-confirm
1. **notify-unauthorized** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the requester hold authority to close this account?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Tell the requester that no closure took place and why this request cannot perform it, without disclosing account state they are not entitled to.
2. **notify-cannot-close** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the requester hold authority to close this account? · Is closure permitted now?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Name the state that prevents closure, without inventing a route around it.
3. **confirm-closure** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the requester hold authority to close this account? · Is closure permitted now? · Did the closure take effect?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Confirm the account relationship is closed and state plainly what closure is not - it is not subscription cancellation and it is not data deletion, both of which have their own lifecycles and their own evidence.
4. **surface** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the requester hold authority to close this account? · Is closure permitted now?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Surface the exact blocker.
   - destination: `closure-blocker` · bound to `closure_request_id` · must not claim: a route around the blocker that does not exist

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [invalid-state] closure not requested by an authorised party; account unchanged (re-entry: a request from an authorised party is assessed on its own terms)
- [failure] one blocker cleared, others remain and are surfaced (re-entry: each remaining blocker is named and resolved on its own, and closure is requested again - stacking them into one message tells the account holder nothing they can act on)
- [timeout] closure request lapsed with blockers outstanding (re-entry: a fresh request re-enters against whatever blockers still stand)
- [failure] closure not permitted (re-entry: the preventing condition changing re-opens this. The account stays open and the reason is on record rather than left as an unexplained refusal)
- [handoff] Account Closure Reconciliation — a closure that has taken effect on the account itself
- [handoff] Ownership Escalation — a closure that did not fully apply

- `account_closure.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per the account and this closure request; a later instance concerns a different the account and this closure request and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
Account closure ends an account relationship only after obligations that legitimately block closure have been resolved.

### Entity (notes)
- the account and this closure request
- Closing the account ends the account relationship. It cancels no subscription and deletes no data, and neither of those happens as a side effect of it.

### Distinct from
- RET-29 (Cancellation completed → stop retention → resolve remaining relationship) — RET-29 completes a subscription cancellation - a commercial relationship ending. An account can be closed with subscriptions running, and a subscription can be cancelled with the account intact.

### Guardrails
1. Account closure is not data deletion.
2. Account closure is not subscription cancellation unless a contract explicitly couples them.
3. Closure does not silently erase audit or history.
4. A blocker is named specifically. An unexplained refusal is not a blocker, it is a support case.

### Technical logic (graph, 19 nodes)
- **t.requested** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `account_closure_requested`
  - Account closure requested
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: a closure request against an identified account
  - not enough on its own: a cancelled subscription, which ends a commercial relationship rather than the account
  - not enough on its own: a data deletion request, which is a different lifecycle with a different authority
  - not enough on its own: a long period of inactivity
  - -> a.state (node)
- **a.state** [action]
  - Record CLOSURE_REQUESTED, and determine who requested it, whether they hold the authority to, the account's current state, its open blockers and its dependent relationships
  - writes closure_log (append)
  - -> c.authority (node)
- **c.authority** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the requester hold authority to close this account?
  - -> c.blockers [Authorised]: they own the account or hold the role that may close it (node)
  - -> a.notify-unauthorized [Not authorised]: the request comes from someone who cannot make it (node)
- **c.blockers** [condition] (3 branches)
  - Is closure permitted now?
  - -> a.execute [Permitted]: no blocker stands in the way (node)
  - -> a.surface [Blocked, recoverable]: an active financial obligation, an open transaction, a required return, a security review, a dependent entity or an administrative process stands in the way and can be resolved (node)
  - -> a.notify-cannot-close [Blocked, not recoverable]: something prevents closure that the account holder cannot resolve (node)
- **a.notify-unauthorized** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the requester that no closure took place and why this request cannot perform it, without disclosing account state they are not entitled to. A closure request that disappears leaves the requester believing an account is closed when it is not
  - -> x.unauthorized (node)
- **a.execute** [action]
  - Execute the account closure. This ends the account relationship and does nothing else - it cancels no subscription unless a contract explicitly couples them, and it deletes no data. The audit and history are untouched
  - writes closure_log (append)
  - -> a.verify (node)
- **a.surface** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Surface the exact blocker. Telling someone they cannot close their account without saying why produces a support case rather than a closure, and it reads as an obstacle rather than a requirement
  - writes closure_log (append)
  - -> w.blockers (node)
- **a.notify-cannot-close** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Name the state that prevents closure, without inventing a route around it. Where nothing the requester can do would change it, saying so is the answer - an unresolvable block reported as silence reads as a request still being processed
  - -> x.cannot-close (node)
- **x.unauthorized** [exit]
  - closure not requested by an authorised party; account unchanged
  - Detail: a request from an authorised party is assessed on its own terms
- **a.verify** [action]
  - Verify the account is no longer active for the operations closure prohibits
  - writes closure_log (append)
  - -> c.verified (node)
- **w.blockers** [wait]
  - until the blocker that prevented closure is resolved
  - Detail: timeout after The closure request's validity window. (configure account_closure.blockers)
  - a closure request held open indefinitely against an unresolved blocker is neither a closure nor a refusal, and the account holder cannot tell which they have
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.recheck [on event] (node)
  - -> x.lapsed [on timeout] (node)
- **x.cannot-close** [exit]
  - closure not permitted
  - Detail: the preventing condition changing re-opens this. The account stays open and the reason is on record rather than left as an unexplained refusal
- **c.verified** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Did the closure take effect?
  - -> a.confirm-closure [Closed]: the account no longer permits prohibited operations (node)
  - -> h.escalate [Not fully applied]: the account remains active somewhere (node)
- **c.recheck** [condition] (2 branches)
  - With that blocker resolved, is anything else in the way?
  - -> a.execute [All clear]: no blocker remains (node, back-edge)
  - -> x.still-blocked [Further blockers]: resolving one revealed or left others (node)
- **x.lapsed** [exit]
  - closure request lapsed with blockers outstanding
  - Detail: a fresh request re-enters against whatever blockers still stand
- **a.confirm-closure** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Confirm the account relationship is closed and state plainly what closure is not - it is not subscription cancellation and it is not data deletion, both of which have their own lifecycles and their own evidence. Sent only after closure is verified, never on the request
  - -> h.dependencies (node)
- **h.escalate** [handoff]
  - Responsibility escalation → higher authority → resolution or return
  - Detail: a closure that did not fully apply
  - carries: where the account remains active
  - carries: the closure request and its authority
  - -> OWN-55 (journey)
- **x.still-blocked** [exit]
  - one blocker cleared, others remain and are surfaced
  - Detail: each remaining blocker is named and resolved on its own, and closure is requested again - stacking them into one message tells the account holder nothing they can act on
- **h.dependencies** [handoff]
  - Account closure → reconcile external and commercial dependencies → finalize
  - Detail: a closure that has taken effect on the account itself
  - carries: the closed account and the dependent relationships identified at request time
  - carries: the explicit fact that no external or commercial dependency has been terminated by this
  - -> TRM-107 (journey)
