**Purpose**: Turn a refund request into an authorised decision, without money moving on the request itself.

### Reusable rule
A refund request creates a financial decision process; money should move only after the request reaches an authorized outcome.

### Entity (notes)
- the refund request and the original transaction it is made against
- The request does not modify the original payment history. It creates a decision process alongside it.

### Distinct from
- FIN-138 (Refund approved → execute → confirm or reconcile) — Approving a refund authorises the movement. FIN-138 performs it and confirms the money arrived, which fails independently and more often than the decision does.

### Guardrails
1. A refund requested is not a refund owed.
2. A refund request does not modify the original payment history.
3. Eligibility policy is never invented. Where none exists the decision goes to a person.

### Technical logic (graph, 17 nodes)
- **t.requested** [trigger] (entry, evidence: declared)
  - Event id: `refund_requested`
  - Refund requested
  - evidence: declared
  - requires: a refund requested against an identified original transaction
  - not enough on its own: dissatisfaction expressed about a purchase, which is feedback until it is made as a request
  - -> a.record (node)
- **a.record** [action]
  - Record the request id, the original transaction, the requested amount, the reason, the requester and the submission time. The original payment history is not modified by a request made against it
  - writes refund_log (append)
  - -> c.scope (node)
- **c.scope** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is the request valid and within the process scope?
  - -> c.policy [Valid]: it identifies a real transaction, from someone entitled to ask, within any applicable window (node)
  - -> a.reject-scope [Out of scope]: it names no valid transaction, comes from someone with no standing, or falls outside the process entirely (node)
- **c.policy** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is a refund eligibility policy defined for this transaction type?
  - -> c.eligible [Defined]: policy states what qualifies for a refund and on what terms (node)
  - -> h.undefined [Not defined]: no policy covers this kind of refund (node)
- **a.reject-scope** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Reject with the actual reason and whatever process does apply. A rejection that does not say why produces the next request rather than closing this one
  - writes refund_log (append)
  - -> x.rejected (node)
- **c.eligible** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What does the policy determine?
  - -> a.approve [Eligible]: the policy's conditions are met deterministically (node)
  - -> a.reject [Ineligible]: the policy rules it out deterministically (node)
  - -> a.review [Requires judgement]: the policy leaves this case to a decision rather than a rule (node)
- **h.undefined** [handoff]
  - Decision request → validate → route, reject or hold
  - Detail: a refund request with no governing eligibility policy
  - carries: the request and the original transaction
  - carries: the explicit fact that no eligibility rule was invented in order to decide it
  - -> DEC-181 (journey)
- **x.rejected** [exit]
  - REJECTED; no money moves and the original transaction stands
  - Detail: a differently grounded request, or new evidence, is assessed on its own. A refund requested is not a refund owed
- **a.approve** [action] (execution: human)
  - Record APPROVED with the authorised amount and the authority that approved it. Approved authorises the movement and performs none of it
  - writes refund_log (append)
  - -> h.execute (node)
- **a.reject** [action]
  - Record REJECTED with the reason drawn from the policy that ruled it out
  - writes refund_log (append)
  - -> a.notify-rejection (node)
- **a.review** [action] (execution: human)
  - Record UNDER_REVIEW and gather whatever the decision requires. Nothing moves while it is under review
  - writes refund_log (append)
  - -> a.acknowledge-review (node)
- **h.execute** [handoff]
  - Refund approved → execute → confirm or reconcile
  - Detail: a refund authorised for execution
  - carries: the approved amount, its currency and the original transaction
  - carries: the explicit fact that nothing has moved yet
  - -> FIN-138 (journey)
- **a.notify-rejection** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the requester the refund was refused and the governing reason, whether that came from policy directly or from a reviewer. A refusal recorded and never sent leaves someone waiting on a decision that has already been made
  - -> x.rejected (node, back-edge)
- **a.acknowledge-review** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the requester the refund is under review and what that state means, without implying an outcome. The wait for a human decision is the longest silence in this journey and the one most easily read as no answer coming
  - -> w.decision (node)
- **w.decision** [wait]
  - until an authorised decision is recorded on the request
  - Detail: timeout after The decision SLA. (configure refund_request.decision)
  - a refund request left undecided is a customer waiting on money with no answer, which produces a dispute that costs more than the refund
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.decision [on event] (node)
  - -> h.escalate [on timeout] (node)
- **c.decision** [condition] (2 branches)
  - What was decided?
  - -> a.approve [Approved]: the reviewer authorised the refund (node, back-edge)
  - -> a.reject [Rejected]: the reviewer declined it (node, back-edge)
- **h.escalate** [handoff]
  - Responsibility escalation → higher authority → resolution or return
  - Detail: a refund decision outliving its SLA
  - carries: the request, its age and what is holding the decision
  - -> OWN-55 (journey)
