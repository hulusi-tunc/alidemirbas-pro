## CON-272 — Contact Recovery

- Canonical ID: `CON-272`
- Slug: `contactability-repair`
- Display name: Contact Recovery
- Canonical name (chain form): Contact point unreachable → alternate route → corrected or suppressed
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/contactability-repair`
- Category: Consent, preferences, communication & contactability
- Goal: delivery-confirmation
- Channels: in-app, sms, email
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Get a dead destination replaced by asking on a route that still works, so a delivery failure is repaired once rather than retried blind - and without either side mistaking it for a change of permission.

> Get a dead destination replaced by asking on a route that still works, so a delivery failure is repaired once rather than retried blind - and without either side mistaking it for a change of permission.

### Trigger
- Event: `contact_point_recorded_undeliverable`
- Meaning: a permanent delivery failure or invalid-destination result recorded against one specific contact point; a failure class that marks the destination unusable rather than temporarily unavailable
- Not enough on its own: a single soft failure or a full mailbox; a message that went unopened; an opt-out, which is a permission fact and says nothing about the route
- Requires: a permanent delivery failure or invalid-destination result recorded against one specific contact point; a failure class that marks the destination unusable rather than temporarily unavailable
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the person plus the one contact point that failed - this address, this number, this token
- instance: contact_point_id + person_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a permanent delivery failure or invalid-destination result recorded against one specific contact point
- a failure class that marks the destination unusable rather than temporarily unavailable
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the person plus the one contact point that failed
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Undeliverable is not opted out, and nothing here changes what may be sent.
- [canonical rule] The repair request never goes to the destination being repaired.
- [canonical rule] An available alternative route is not permission to use it. Availability is the easiest thing to check and the least meaningful.
- [canonical rule] One repair cycle per destination. A dead route asked twice is still dead and the second ask is paid for in delivery reputation.
- [canonical rule] A corrected value is verified before the destination is treated as usable, and nothing held is replayed on it.

### Recommended orchestration · offer-decide-remind
1. **prompt-in-app** (service, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: What can carry the repair request without using the broken destination?
   - Channel roles: in-session
   - Purpose: Ask for a corrected destination where the person already is, naming which one stopped working and what is being held because of it.
   - destination: `contact-point-update` · bound to `contact_point_id`
2. **prompt-alt** (service, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: What can carry the repair request without using the broken destination?
   - Channel roles: urgent -> persistent
   - Purpose: Ask for a corrected destination on the surviving permitted route, naming the one that failed.
   - destination: `contact-point-update` · bound to `contact_point_id`
3. **confirm** (service, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - Timing: The single repair cycle allowed for this destination. (configure contactability_repair.corrected) (after the previous touch)
   - cancelled by a replacement destination is supplied and verified; the original destination is deliverable again; the person removes the destination
   - re-read before sending: the the person plus the one contact point that failed re-read from the system of record before acting on the timeout
   - checks: What ended the wait?
   - Channel roles: in-session -> persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Confirm on the working route that the replacement is now in use, and say that this changed where things go and not what may be sent.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- urgent (sms) — an asserted time bound lies inside the urgent horizon and permission for messages on this channel is recorded
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [suppression] no working permitted route; the destination stays suppressed (re-entry: if any route becomes deliverable and permitted, the repair request runs from there)
- [success] destination replaced and in use; permission unchanged (re-entry: a later failure on any destination is its own instance)
- [success] original destination reachable again; nothing replayed (re-entry: a further failure on the same destination re-enters here with a new repair cycle)
- [suppression] destination stays suppressed after one repair cycle (re-entry: a corrected destination supplied later re-enters at confirmation, once it has verified)

### Reusable rule
You cannot ask somebody to fix a broken route by sending the request down it.

### Entity (notes)
- the person plus the one contact point that failed - this address, this number, this token
- The failure belongs to the destination, not to the person and not to the channel class. A second destination failing is its own instance and gets its own repair cycle.

### Distinct from
- CON-36 (Channel contactability change → recalculate reachability → route or suppress) — CON-36 holds reachability per destination and decides what is suppressed. This journey is the single request to the person that could change that, and it only ever runs on a route CON-36 has already found healthy and permitted.
- CON-38 (Communication suppression → reason → release or persist) — CON-38 governs suppression by reason, including permission-based ones. Here the destination is suppressed for a technical failure only, and nothing about what may be sent has changed.
- CON-264 (Contact point added or changed → confirm → permitted or lapsed) — CON-264 confirms a destination that was added or changed through the person's own action. A destination this journey's own a.confirm has already verified came from a repair, not a self-service change, and is not independently re-confirmed by CON-264 for the same event - this journey's change_source marks the origin so CON-264 can tell the two apart.

### Guardrails
1. Undeliverable is not opted out, and nothing here changes what may be sent.
2. The repair request never goes to the destination being repaired.
3. An available alternative route is not permission to use it. Availability is the easiest thing to check and the least meaningful.
4. One repair cycle per destination. A dead route asked twice is still dead and the second ask is paid for in delivery reputation.
5. A corrected value is verified before the destination is treated as usable, and nothing held is replayed on it.
