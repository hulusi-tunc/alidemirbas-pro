## CON-283 — Frequency Preference Update

- Canonical ID: `CON-283`
- Slug: `frequency-reduction-confirmation`
- Display name: Frequency Preference Update
- Canonical name (chain form): Frequency reduced → cadence recalculated → kept rather than lost
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/frequency-reduction-confirmation`
- Category: Consent, preferences, communication & contactability
- Goal: consent-permission
- Channels: email
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Confirm to somebody who asked for less that less is what they will get, so that asking for fewer messages stays a real alternative to asking for none.

> Confirm to somebody who asked for less that less is what they will get, so that asking for fewer messages stays a real alternative to asking for none.

### Trigger
- Event: `frequency_preference_reduced`
- Meaning: an authoritative frequency preference recorded against the person; the new cadence expressed as something that can actually be applied; the classes it governs
- Not enough on its own: a complaint about volume with no preference recorded; a period of no engagement
- Requires: an authoritative frequency preference recorded against the person; the new cadence expressed as something that can actually be applied; the classes it governs
- Source: declared

### Entity
- Scope: the person and the optional communication classes the reduced frequency actually governs
- instance: person_id · one-active-per-key

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Fewer is not none. A reduction that is enforced as a stop loses the relationship the person was trying to keep.
- [canonical rule] Required and transactional communication stays governed by its own rules, whatever the preference says.
- [canonical rule] One confirmation, and it is the last message at the old cadence rather than the first at the new one.
- [canonical rule] A cadence change is prospective. What was already delivered is not revisited.

### Recommended orchestration · single-notice

   - checks: Did they ask for less, or for none? · Does what is already scheduled exceed the new cadence?
   - Channel roles: persistent
   - Purpose: Confirm once what changed: how often optional communication will now arrive, what is unaffected because it was never optional, and the route to stopping it altogether.
   - destination: `preference-centre` · bound to `person_id` · must not claim: that required or transactional communication changes

**Channel roles** (recommended default)

- [success] reduced cadence in effect and holding (re-entry: a later change to the same preference starts a new instance)

### Reusable rule
Somebody asking for less is staying, and the only way to lose them is to hear it as leaving.

### Entity (notes)
- the person and the optional communication classes the reduced frequency actually governs
- The governed set is the whole question. A frequency preference that quietly reaches required communication is an opt-out nobody chose.

### Distinct from
- CON-34 (Communication frequency change → recalculate cadence → apply prospectively) — CON-34 works out which classes the preference governs and recalculates the cadence. This journey is the single confirmation the person receives, and it states nothing the recalculation has not already applied.
- CON-38 (Communication suppression → reason → release or persist) — CON-38 records a stop and decides when it releases. A reduction is not a stop, and recording the two as the same state loses exactly the relationship the person was trying to keep.

### Guardrails
1. Fewer is not none. A reduction that is enforced as a stop loses the relationship the person was trying to keep.
2. Required and transactional communication stays governed by its own rules, whatever the preference says.
3. One confirmation, and it is the last message at the old cadence rather than the first at the new one.
4. A cadence change is prospective. What was already delivered is not revisited.

  - -> c.less-or-none (node)
- **c.less-or-none** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Did they ask for less, or for none?
  - -> a.recalculate [Fewer]: the preference sets a reduced cadence for optional communication rather than ending it (node)
  - -> x.opted-out [None at all]: the preference is a full withdrawal from optional communication (node)
- **a.recalculate** [action]
  - Recalculate the forward cadence for the optional classes only, leaving required and transactional communication under its own rules. Somebody asking to hear from us less often has not asked to stop being told about their own obligations
  - -> c.queued (node)
- **x.opted-out** [exit]
  - full opt-out; this journey sends nothing
  - Detail: if a reduced cadence is later chosen instead of none, that qualifies here again
- **c.queued** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does what is already scheduled exceed the new cadence?
  - -> a.trim [Exceeds it]: optional communication is already queued at a rate the new cadence does not allow (node)
  - -> a.confirm [Within it]: what is scheduled already fits the new cadence (node)
- **a.trim** [action]
  - Suppress or reschedule the optional communication that now exceeds the cadence, choosing between the two by whether the message still means anything later. The first thing somebody receives after asking for less must not be the backlog
  - -> a.confirm (node)
- **a.confirm** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Confirm once what changed: how often optional communication will now arrive, what is unaffected because it was never optional, and the route to stopping it altogether. Naming what is unaffected is what stops the person concluding that nothing was applied when a required notice arrives next week
  - -> w.cycle (node)
- **w.cycle** [wait]
  - until a deliberate change to how often communication should arrive - weekly to monthly, everything to important only, a reduction to a stated minimum, or the person withdraws from all optional communication
  - Detail: timeout after One full cycle at the new cadence. (configure frequency_reduction.cycle)
  - a preference that has survived a cycle is the settled state, and holding the journey open past that invents an interest in the preference that nobody has
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.settled [on event] (node)
  - -> x.holding [on timeout] (node)
- **c.settled** [condition] (2 branches)
  - What changed during the first cycle?
  - -> x.reduced-again [Reduced again]: a further reduction was recorded before the cycle completed (node)
  - -> x.opted-out [Stopped altogether]: a full withdrawal was recorded before the cycle completed (node, back-edge)
- **x.holding** [exit]
  - reduced cadence in effect and holding
  - Detail: a later change to the same preference starts a new instance
- **x.reduced-again** [exit]
  - reduced a second time before the first cadence settled
  - Detail: each recorded reduction is its own instance and gets its own single confirmation
