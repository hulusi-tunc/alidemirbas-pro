## IDN-270 — Account Recovery

- Canonical ID: `IDN-270`
- Slug: `account-recovery-guidance`
- Display name: Account Recovery
- Canonical name (chain form): Account recovery started → proof of control → access restored or window closed
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/account-recovery-guidance`
- Category: Identity, verification, authentication & account integrity
- Goal: suspension-restoration
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Carry somebody who cannot authenticate through the evidence their recovery actually requires, inside a stated window, on a route that is no weaker than the login it is standing in for.

> Carry somebody who cannot authenticate through the evidence their recovery actually requires, inside a stated window, on a route that is no weaker than the login it is standing in for.

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] The recovery route goes to a destination the account already held, never to one supplied with the request.
- [canonical rule] Recovery is a different route to the same assurance, not a lower one. Nothing here is easier than the authentication it stands in for.
- [canonical rule] The window is never restarted by repeated attempts, and never extended by engagement.
- [canonical rule] One reminder, never two. On a security route, repetition is indistinguishable from pressure.
- [canonical rule] What was invalidated is named in the confirmation, or the next refused credential reads as a fresh attack.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- urgent (sms) — an asserted time bound lies inside the urgent horizon and permission for messages on this channel is recorded
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] control restored and confirmed (re-entry: a later recovery case on the same account is a new instance with its own window)
- [success] recovery closed without being used (re-entry: a new request opens a new case; this one is not resumed)
- [timeout] recovery window closed without sufficient proof (re-entry: a fresh request opens a new case and a new window; the closed one is never extended)
- [handoff] Account Compromise Recovery — a recovery attempt on an account that is already under an open compromise signal

- `account_recovery.final` [config required] (attribute-bound): configure account_recovery.final — After the reminder the instance waits until the recovery window itself closes; the window is never restarted.
- `account_recovery.touches` [recommended default]: 1 — Every touch runs against a budget fixed when the instance opened; the budget is the plan's own length, and no touch is repeated because nothing could tell whether it arrived.
- `account_recovery.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per one existing account plus this recovery case and its window; a later instance concerns a different one existing account plus this recovery case and its window and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
A way back into an account is only worth offering if it is no easier to walk than the door it replaces.

### Entity (notes)
- one existing account plus this recovery case and its window
- One case, one window. A second recovery request on the same account opens its own case; it does not extend or restart this one.

### Distinct from
- IDN-88 (Account recovery request → prove control → restore secure access) — IDN-88 decides the recovery basis, judges the evidence and rebuilds secure access. This journey sends nothing that IDN-88 has not already established, and it decides nothing - it is the requester's side of that case.
- IDN-90 (Suspected account compromise → contain → verify → recover or clear) — IDN-90 governs a suspected compromise. Here the account is not under suspicion; somebody simply cannot get in, and treating the two the same makes every locked-out person a suspect.

### Guardrails
1. The recovery route goes to a destination the account already held, never to one supplied with the request.
2. Recovery is a different route to the same assurance, not a lower one. Nothing here is easier than the authentication it stands in for.
3. The window is never restarted by repeated attempts, and never extended by engagement.
4. One reminder, never two. On a security route, repetition is indistinguishable from pressure.
5. What was invalidated is named in the confirmation, or the next refused credential reads as a fresh attack.

- **c.proof** [condition] (2 branches)

- **c.remind** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is one reminder still worth sending?

  - -> x.closed [Effectively closed]: too little of the window remains for the evidence to arrive in time (node)
- **a.restored** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Confirm that control is back and name what was invalidated on the way - the sessions and credentials that will no longer work. Somebody who is not told what was cut off reads the next refusal as a second compromise
  - -> x.restored (node)
- **x.moot** [exit]

  - Detail: a new request opens a new case; this one is not resumed
- **a.remind** [action] (execution: communication)

- **x.closed** [exit]
  - recovery window closed without sufficient proof
  - Detail: a fresh request opens a new case and a new window; the closed one is never extended
- **x.restored** [exit]
  - control restored and confirmed
  - Detail: a later recovery case on the same account is a new instance with its own window
- **w.final** [wait]
  - until sufficient proof of control or identity is provided
  - Detail: timeout after After the reminder the instance waits until the recovery window itself closes; the window is never restarted. (configure account_recovery.final)
  - the window is a security control rather than a courtesy, and a route left open indefinitely is weaker than the login it replaced
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.proof [on event] (node, back-edge)
  - -> x.closed [on timeout] (node, back-edge)
