## ACC-261 — Access Restriction Notice

- Canonical ID: `ACC-261`
- Slug: `access-restriction-route-back`
- Display name: Access Restriction Notice
- Canonical name (chain form): Access restricted or ending → stated route back → restored or ends
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/access-restriction-route-back`
- Category: Access, entitlement, credentials & capability
- Goal: suspension-restoration
- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Tell the person holding the account what access is going away, when, and the one condition that would bring it back - so a restriction is a decision they can act on rather than a discovery they make later.

> Tell the person holding the account what access is going away, when, and the one condition that would bring it back - so a restriction is a decision they can act on rather than a discovery they make later.

### Trigger
- Event: `access_restriction_or_end_recorded`
- Meaning: an authoritative restriction, suspension or end date recorded against the account; a stated condition or deadline that would resolve it
- Not enough on its own: a risk signal that has not yet produced a restriction; an internal review that has not concluded
- Requires: an authoritative restriction, suspension or end date recorded against the account; a stated condition or deadline that would resolve it
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: person or account plus the specific restriction or end date placed on it
- instance: account_id + restriction_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- an authoritative restriction, suspension or end date recorded against the account
- a stated condition or deadline that would resolve it
- no instance of this journey is already open for the person or account plus the specific restriction or end date placed on it
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] A restriction is never announced before it is authoritatively recorded. Warning about a decision nobody has taken is how a support queue fills up.
- [canonical rule] The notice names what still works, not only what stopped.
- [canonical rule] Where the holder cannot resolve the condition, no call to action is attached.
- [canonical rule] Restoration is confirmed explicitly. Silence after a resolved restriction reads as the restriction continuing.

### Recommended orchestration · offer-decide-remind
1. **inform-only** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Can the holder do anything about this?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Tell them what is restricted and until when, with no call to action attached - because there is nothing for them to do.
2. **notify** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Can the holder do anything about this? · Is there a permitted route to them?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: State exactly what is restricted, what still works, the deadline, and the single condition that lifts it.
   - destination: `resolution-condition` · bound to `restriction_id` · must not claim: a restriction that is not yet authoritative
3. **confirm** (service, canonical rule)
   - Timing: The stated deadline or review point. (configure access_restriction.resolve) (relative to resolution_deadline_at)
   - cancelled by the condition recorded to release the suppression is met; the restriction is lifted by its owner; the restriction is made permanent
   - re-read before sending: the person or account plus the specific restriction or end date placed on it re-read from the system of record before acting on the timeout
   - checks: Where did it land?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Confirm that access is back and name what was restored, so the person can tell the difference between a resolved restriction and a partial one
   - after t2

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] informed, resolution not theirs (re-entry: if the condition later becomes something they can satisfy, this qualifies again with the actionable path)
- [suppression] restriction announced by the security response; no separate notice sent (re-entry: the security response clearing, or converting the restriction into an ordinary one, re-evaluates it here on its own terms)
- [success] restored and confirmed (re-entry: a later restriction on the same account is a new instance)
- [timeout] restriction stands past its deadline (re-entry: if the condition is satisfied afterwards, the restoration path runs from the lifting event)
- [handoff] Contactability Recalculation — a restriction notice that cannot be delivered on any permitted route

### Reusable rule
A restriction told to somebody names its own release condition, or it is a punishment rather than a decision.

### Entity (notes)
- person or account plus the specific restriction or end date placed on it
- The restriction is the subject. A second, unrelated restriction on the same account is its own instance and gets its own notice.

### Distinct from
- ACC-78 (Access suspension → restricted state → restore or terminate) — ACC-78 decides and records the restriction and its release condition. This journey is what the account holder is told about it, and it sends nothing until that decision exists.
- ACC-74 (Entitlement loss → revoke future capability → reconcile existing obligations) — ACC-74 ends future use of a lost entitlement. Here the point is the window before that takes effect, which is the only period in which the person can still act.

### Guardrails
1. A restriction is never announced before it is authoritatively recorded. Warning about a decision nobody has taken is how a support queue fills up.
2. The notice names what still works, not only what stopped.
3. Where the holder cannot resolve the condition, no call to action is attached.
4. Restoration is confirmed explicitly. Silence after a resolved restriction reads as the restriction continuing.
