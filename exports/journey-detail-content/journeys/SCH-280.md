## SCH-280 — No-Show Follow-Up

- Canonical ID: `SCH-280`
- Slug: `no-show-rebooking`
- Display name: No-Show Follow-Up
- Canonical name (chain form): No-show confirmed → validate the miss → rebook or close
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/no-show-rebooking`
- Category: Scheduling, appointments, reservations & time-bound commitments
- Goal: scheduling-commitment
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Offer a way back to somebody whose booking did not happen, having first established that the miss was theirs and not ours - because a rebooking prompt sent over our own failure is an accusation.

> After a genuinely missed booking, state the fact without penalty language and give the single route to a new booking where one exists - after re-reading the booking, and never where our side could not have delivered.

### Trigger
- Event: `no_show_recorded_against_booking`
- Meaning: a no-show authoritatively recorded against one confirmed booking; the semantics it was judged under and the window that closed; policy naming rebooking rather than a fee as what follows
- Not enough on its own: a service window that has passed with no attendance event yet recorded; a provider running late; a booking with no arrival semantics defined
- Requires: a no-show authoritatively recorded against one confirmed booking; the semantics it was judged under and the window that closed; policy naming rebooking rather than a fee as what follows
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the single booking occurrence that did not take place
- instance: booking_id + occurrence_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a no-show is recorded against one booking occurrence by the attendance source
- the booking, re-read after the recording lag, genuinely did not happen - not moved, cancelled or attended after all
- the provider or resource could have delivered; a failure on our side goes to reschedule, not follow-up
- hard gates (GLB-31) permit service communication

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Attendance and cancellation events lag; the booking's latest events are re-read before anything leaves, and a booking moved, cancelled or attended after all sends nothing.
- [canonical rule] A missed booking the provider or resource could not have delivered is ours, not the customer's; it goes to reschedule and no follow-up is sent.
- [canonical rule] The miss is stated as a fact; no penalty language, and no penalty applied here - the commitment's own terms own consequences.
- [canonical rule] Where nothing can be rebooked, the notice acknowledges the miss and what it means, with no rebooking prompt attached.
- [canonical rule] Hard gates (GLB-31) apply; pressure caps do not, because this concerns a commitment the person made.

### Recommended orchestration · single-notice
1. **rebooking-offer** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Did the booking genuinely not happen? · Could the provider or resource actually have delivered? · Is there anything to offer?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Say that the booking was missed as a fact, without penalty language, and give the single route to a new one within the stated window.
   - destination: `rebooking` · bound to `booking_id` · must not claim: a penalty, that a slot is held
2. **acknowledgement** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Did the booking genuinely not happen? · Could the provider or resource actually have delivered? · Is there anything to offer?
   - Channel roles: persistent
   - Purpose: State plainly that the booking did not happen and what that means, with no rebooking prompt attached because there is nothing to rebook onto.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the offer carries the rebooking route and should be kept - the default
- urgent (sms) — permission for service messages on the channel is recorded and the rebooking window is short
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [suppression] nothing sent; the booking was moved, cancelled or attended after all (re-entry: a later booking that is genuinely missed is its own instance)
- [success] rebooked (re-entry: the new booking has its own lifecycle and a later miss enters here again)
- [timeout] closed with no rebooking (re-entry: a request from the customer later reopens booking through the ordinary route, not through this one)
- [handoff] Booking Reschedule — a missed booking the provider or resource could not have delivered

### Configure
- `no_show.rebooking_window` [recommended default] (response-window): 7 days–14 days — The offer stands for the window stated in it; when the window passes the instance closes and the offer is not repeated.
- `no_show.touches` [recommended default]: 1 — One follow-up per missed occurrence - an offer or an acknowledgement, never both; an unanswered offer is not repeated.
- `no_show.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — Follow-up is per occurrence; a later miss is its own instance, though repeated misses are evidence the commitment's own policy reads.

### Reusable rule
Before telling somebody they missed something, prove it was not us who missed it.

### Entity (notes)
- the single booking occurrence that did not take place
- One occurrence. This says nothing about the person's history or standing - it is a fact about one booking, and a second miss is its own instance.

### Distinct from
- SCH-179 (No-show or missed appointment → validate → rebook, close or consequence) — SCH-179 establishes and records the no-show and decides what consequence policy attaches. This journey is what the customer is told, it never creates the record, and it runs only where policy attached rebooking rather than a consequence.
- SCH-177 (Scheduled time approaches → revalidate → check in, start or exception) — SCH-177 runs as the time approaches and is about getting somebody there. This starts only once the window has closed with nobody there.

### Guardrails
1. The record is re-read immediately before sending. A message about a miss is the one thing that cannot be sent on stale state.
2. Provider-side failure is ruled out before the customer is told anything about a miss.
3. No blame, no penalty language, no history. This is one booking.
4. One offer, not a sequence. Somebody who missed once is not pursued.

### Technical logic (graph, 13 nodes)
- **t.no-show** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `no_show_recorded_against_booking`
  - No show recorded against booking
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: a no-show authoritatively recorded against one confirmed booking
  - requires: the semantics it was judged under and the window that closed
  - requires: policy naming rebooking rather than a fee as what follows
  - not enough on its own: a service window that has passed with no attendance event yet recorded
  - not enough on its own: a provider running late
  - not enough on its own: a booking with no arrival semantics defined
  - -> a.reread (node)
- **a.reread** [action]
  - Re-read the booking's latest events before anything leaves the building. Attendance and cancellation events land late, and the gap between the record being written and the message being sent is exactly where a correct cancellation gets treated as a miss
  - -> c.superseded (node)
- **c.superseded** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Did the booking genuinely not happen?
  - -> x.suppressed [Superseded or reconciled]: a cancellation, a reschedule or a late attendance event has landed since the no-show was recorded (node)
  - -> c.provider [Genuinely missed]: the record still stands after the re-read (node)
- **x.suppressed** [exit]
  - nothing sent; the booking was moved, cancelled or attended after all
  - Detail: a later booking that is genuinely missed is its own instance
- **c.provider** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Could the provider or resource actually have delivered?
  - -> c.rebookable [Deliverable]: the provider and the resource were available and able to deliver for the whole window (node)
  - -> h.provider [Our side failed]: the provider, the resource or the location could not have delivered the booking as confirmed (node)
- **c.rebookable** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is there anything to offer?
  - -> a.offer [Rebooking available]: the same commitment can still be met and capacity for it exists (node)
  - -> a.acknowledge [Nothing to rebook onto]: the commitment has lapsed, the entitlement behind it has gone, or no capacity remains (node)
- **h.provider** [handoff]
  - Provider or resource cancellation → reallocate → reschedule, remedy or cancel
  - Detail: a missed booking the provider or resource could not have delivered
  - carries: the window that closed and what was already said to the customer about it
  - carries: the evidence that delivery was not possible on our side
  - -> SCH-180 (journey)
- **a.offer** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say that the booking was missed as a fact, without penalty language, and give the single route to a new one with the date that route closes. Attaching blame to the miss makes rebooking a confession, and people do not book to confess
  - -> w.rebook (node)
- **a.acknowledge** [action] (execution: communication)
  - State plainly that the booking did not happen and what that means, with no rebooking prompt attached because there is nothing to book. An offer with nothing behind it costs more trust than saying nothing would
  - -> x.closed (node)
- **w.rebook** [wait]
  - until a new booking is created for the same commitment, or the customer declines a further booking
  - Detail: timeout after The offer stands for the window stated in it; when the window passes the instance closes and the offer is not repeated. (example: 7 days–14 days; configure no_show.rebooking_window)
  - the stated window is the whole content of the offer, and letting it pass silently makes what was said untrue
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.outcome [on event] (node)
  - -> x.closed [on timeout] (node)
- **x.closed** [exit]
  - closed with no rebooking
  - Detail: a request from the customer later reopens booking through the ordinary route, not through this one
- **c.outcome** [condition] (2 branches)
  - How did the offer resolve?
  - -> x.rebooked [Rebooked]: a new booking exists against the same commitment (node)
  - -> x.closed [Declined]: the customer said they do not want another booking (node, back-edge)
- **x.rebooked** [exit]
  - rebooked
  - Detail: the new booking has its own lifecycle and a later miss enters here again
