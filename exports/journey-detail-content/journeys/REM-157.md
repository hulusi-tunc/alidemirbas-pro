## REM-157 — Remedy Confirmation

- Canonical ID: `REM-157`
- Slug: `remedy-selection`
- Display name: Remedy Confirmation
- Canonical name (chain form): Remedy selection → resolve obligation → financial handoff if needed
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/remedy-selection`
- Category: Returns, remedies, corrections & post-completion recovery
- Goal: compensation-remedy
- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Choose the remedy that would actually satisfy the unresolved obligation, from the ones that genuinely exist.

> Choose the remedy that would actually satisfy the unresolved obligation, from the ones that genuinely exist.

### Trigger
- Event: `remedy_decision_required`
- Meaning: a confirmed issue with an unresolved obligation and no remedy yet selected
- Not enough on its own: a complaint received but not yet established as an operational fault, which is FBK-43's question; a refund request, which presumes the remedy this journey has not yet decided
- Requires: a confirmed issue with an unresolved obligation and no remedy yet selected
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the confirmed issue and the unresolved obligation behind it
- instance: issue_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a confirmed issue with an unresolved obligation and no remedy yet selected
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the confirmed issue and the unresolved obligation behind it
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] A refund is not the universal remedy.
- [canonical rule] Compensation and resolution may be separate, and resolving the obligation does not require compensating for it.
- [canonical rule] Remedies that are not actually available are not offered.
- [canonical rule] The selection starts from the unresolved obligation rather than from the complaint.

### Recommended orchestration · notice-then-confirm
1. **present** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the counterparty choose between remedies?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Present only the options that are genuinely available, with what each would mean
   - destination: `remedy-options` · bound to `issue_id` · must not claim: a remedy that is not actually available
2. **no-remedy** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does the counterparty choose between remedies? · Which remedy resolves the obligation?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: State that the obligation is considered satisfied, or that policy provides no remedy for the facts as confirmed, and name a separate appeal or escalation route only where one actually exists.
   - destination: `appeal-or-escalation-route` · bound to `issue_id`

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] no remedy owed; the obligation is satisfied or none applies (re-entry: new evidence about the obligation re-opens this. Compensation for impact, if any is appropriate, is a separate decision that this outcome does not settle either way)
- [handoff] Corrective Reperformance — correction or reperformance selected
- [handoff] Replacement Fulfillment — replacement selected
- [handoff] Return Request — a return required before the remedy can be settled
- [handoff] Refund Request — a refund or credit selected as the remedy

### Configure
- `remedy_selection.selection` [config required] (response-window): configure remedy_selection.selection — The choice of remedy is waited for a bounded period from the options being presented; an unanswered choice takes the policy default.
- `remedy_selection.touches` [recommended default]: 2 — Every touch runs against a budget fixed when the instance opened; the budget is the plan's own length, and no touch is repeated because nothing could tell whether it arrived.
- `remedy_selection.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per the confirmed issue and the unresolved obligation behind it; a later instance concerns a different the confirmed issue and the unresolved obligation behind it and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
Remedy selection should be driven by the unresolved obligation and available policy rather than by a default compensation mechanism.

### Entity (notes)
- the confirmed issue and the unresolved obligation behind it
- The obligation is the input, not the complaint. What is owed and what someone is upset about are related and not the same, and only the first can be satisfied.

### Distinct from
- FIN-137 (Refund request → eligibility → approve, reject or review) — This decides which remedy applies. FIN-137 runs only if that decision is a refund, and then decides whether the refund is owed - a separate eligibility with separate rules.

### Guardrails
1. A refund is not the universal remedy.
2. Compensation and resolution may be separate, and resolving the obligation does not require compensating for it.
3. Remedies that are not actually available are not offered.
4. The selection starts from the unresolved obligation rather than from the complaint.

### Technical logic (graph, 14 nodes)
- **t.decision** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `remedy_decision_required`
  - Remedy decision required
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: a confirmed issue with an unresolved obligation and no remedy yet selected
  - not enough on its own: a complaint received but not yet established as an operational fault, which is FBK-43's question
  - not enough on its own: a refund request, which presumes the remedy this journey has not yet decided
  - -> a.obligation (node)
- **a.obligation** [action]
  - Identify the unresolved obligation precisely - what was owed that has not been delivered, or what was delivered that is not what was owed. The remedy is chosen to satisfy that rather than to satisfy the complaint, and compensation for the inconvenience is a separate question asked separately
  - writes remedy_log (append)
  - -> a.evaluate (node)
- **a.evaluate** [action]
  - Evaluate the remedies policy actually makes available for this obligation. A remedy that cannot be delivered is not offered - offering one converts a solvable problem into a broken second promise, and the second one costs more than the first
  - -> c.choice (node)
- **c.choice** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the counterparty choose between remedies?
  - -> a.present [They choose]: more than one available remedy would satisfy the obligation and the preference is theirs (node)
  - -> c.route [No choice to make]: one remedy applies, or policy determines it (node)
- **a.present** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Present only the options that are genuinely available, with what each would mean
  - writes remedy_log (append)
  - -> w.selection (node)
- **c.route** [condition] (5 branches)
  - Which remedy resolves the obligation?
  - -> h.correction [Correction or reperformance]: what exists can be made right, or the service can be performed again (node)
  - -> h.replacement [Replacement]: a different instance of the thing is what satisfies the obligation (node)
  - -> h.return [Return, before anything else]: the resource has to come back before a further remedy can be settled, and a return was not already rejected for this issue - a rejected return does not get re-selected when REM-152's own h.alternative routes back here; that path already arrives at c.route with the rejection recorded, and this branch's condition is false the second time (node)
  - -> h.financial [Refund or credit]: money is the remedy the obligation calls for (node)
  - -> a.no-remedy [No remedy is owed]: the obligation turns out to be satisfied, or no remedy applies under policy (node)
- **w.selection** [wait]
  - until the person selects a remedy
  - Detail: timeout after The choice of remedy is waited for a bounded period from the options being presented; an unanswered choice takes the policy default. (configure remedy_selection.selection)
  - an unanswered choice leaves the obligation unresolved, and a default that policy defines is better than an open case waiting on someone who has moved on
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.route [on event] (node, back-edge)
  - -> a.default [on timeout] (node)
- **h.correction** [handoff]

  - carries: the defect and the corrected outcome required
  - carries: the affected scope
  - -> REM-156 (journey)
- **h.replacement** [handoff]

  - Detail: replacement selected
  - carries: the original fulfillment and the defect
  - carries: the replacement scope required
  - -> REM-155 (journey)
- **h.return** [handoff]

  - Detail: a return required before the remedy can be settled
  - carries: the resource to be returned and why

  - -> REM-152 (journey)
- **h.financial** [handoff]
  - Refund request → eligibility → approve, reject or review
  - Detail: a refund or credit selected as the remedy

  - -> FIN-137 (journey)
- **a.no-remedy** [action] (execution: communication)
  - State that the obligation is considered satisfied, or that policy provides no remedy for the facts as confirmed, and name a separate appeal or escalation route only where one actually exists. This journey opens from a confirmed issue - reaching no remedy and saying nothing leaves the person believing the question is still open
  - -> x.no-remedy (node)
- **a.default** [action]
