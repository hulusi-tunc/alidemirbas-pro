## INT-269 — Integration Recovery

- Canonical ID: `INT-269`
- Slug: `integration-reconnect-prompt`
- Display name: Integration Recovery
- Canonical name (chain form): Integration connection broken → reconnect → restored or degraded
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/integration-reconnect-prompt`
- Category: Integrations, synchronization, external systems & data consistency
- Goal: suspension-restoration
- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Tell the person who owns a broken connection which of their capabilities stopped, whether the fix is theirs to perform, and what one step restores it - so a silent dependency failure becomes a decision instead of a slow discovery.

> Tell the person who owns a broken connection which of their capabilities stopped, whether the fix is theirs to perform, and what one step restores it - so a silent dependency failure becomes a decision instead of a slow discovery.

### Trigger
- Event: `integration_connection_failed_or_deauthorized`
- Meaning: an authoritative failed or disconnected state recorded against a named connection; a diagnosed failure cause; the list of capabilities that depended on it
- Not enough on its own: a single failed call that has not been classified; a provider rate limit that clears itself; a scheduled provider maintenance window
- Requires: an authoritative failed or disconnected state recorded against a named connection; a diagnosed failure cause; the list of capabilities that depended on it
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: one connection to one external provider, plus the failure recorded against it
- instance: connection_id + failure_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- an authoritative failed or disconnected state recorded against a named connection
- a diagnosed failure cause
- the list of capabilities that depended on it
- no instance of this journey is already open for the one connection to one external provider
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Nothing is sent before the failure has a diagnosed cause. A notice that cannot say what to do is a notice that generates the question it should have answered.
- [canonical rule] A reduced authorization disables what depended on it, never the whole connection.
- [canonical rule] Where the holder cannot perform the fix, no reconnection step is attached.
- [canonical rule] Restoration is confirmed capability by capability, because a partial recovery and a full one are told apart nowhere else.

2. **partial** (service, canonical rule)

3. **total** (service, canonical rule)

   - checks: Is the fix the holder's to perform? · How much of the connection is affected?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] informed, recovery not the holder's to perform (re-entry: if the cause is later reclassified as holder-fixable, this qualifies again on the re-authorisation path)
- [success] reconnected and confirmed (re-entry: a later break on the same connection is a new instance)
- [invalid-state] connection deliberately removed by the holder (re-entry: connecting the same provider again is a new connection and enters through activation, not through this journey)
- [timeout] recovery window closed, connection still broken, relationship not labelled at risk (re-entry: a further disconnection event, or a re-authorisation attempt, opens recovery again)
- [handoff] Churn Risk Escalation — a connection the holder could re-authorise and has not, past the point where the break is still being treated as an interruption

### Configure
- `integration_reconnect.revalidate` [config required] (response-window): configure integration_reconnect.revalidate — The re-authorisation step is left open for a bounded response window from the notice; a connection still broken past it is judged against independent relationship evidence, never labelled from this break alone.
- `integration_reconnect.touches` [recommended default]: 2 — Every touch runs against a budget fixed when the instance opened; the budget is the plan's own length, and no touch is repeated because nothing could tell whether it arrived.
- `integration_reconnect.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per one connection to one external provider; a later instance concerns a different one connection to one external provider and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
A broken dependency is worth telling somebody about only when the message can name both what stopped and the one act that would restart it.

### Entity (notes)
- one connection to one external provider, plus the failure recorded against it
- The connection is the subject. A second provider failing at the same time is its own instance, and a capability that never depended on this connection is untouched.

### Distinct from
- INT-112 (Integration credential or authorization change → revalidate → continue or degrade) — INT-112 revalidates capabilities against a changed authorization and sets the connection's state. This journey starts once that state says something is broken, and it never decides what is broken - only what the holder is told.
- INT-116 (Integration failure → stop unsafe operations → diagnose → restore) — INT-116 stops unsafe operations and preserves in-flight work. Here nothing operational happens; the subject is the holder's window to re-authorise before the break becomes permanent.

### Guardrails
1. Nothing is sent before the failure has a diagnosed cause. A notice that cannot say what to do is a notice that generates the question it should have answered.
2. A reduced authorization disables what depended on it, never the whole connection.
3. Where the holder cannot perform the fix, no reconnection step is attached.
4. Restoration is confirmed capability by capability, because a partial recovery and a full one are told apart nowhere else.

### Technical logic (graph, 16 nodes)
- **t.disconnected** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `integration_connection_failed_or_deauthorized`
  - Integration connection failed or deauthorized
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: an authoritative failed or disconnected state recorded against a named connection
  - requires: a diagnosed failure cause
  - requires: the list of capabilities that depended on it
  - not enough on its own: a single failed call that has not been classified
  - not enough on its own: a provider rate limit that clears itself
  - not enough on its own: a scheduled provider maintenance window
  - -> c.fixable (node)
- **c.fixable** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is the fix the holder's to perform?
  - -> c.scope [Holder can re-authorise]: the cause is a withdrawn, expired or insufficient authorization on the holder's side of the connection (node)
  - -> a.inform-only [Not theirs]: the cause is a provider outage, a defect on our side, or an entitlement the holder does not control (node)
- **c.scope** [condition] (2 branches)
  - How much of the connection is affected?
  - -> a.partial [Some capabilities]: the authorization still covers part of what was granted and the rest of the connection keeps running (node)
  - -> a.total [Nothing works]: no capability on this connection can run until it is re-authorised (node)
- **a.inform-only** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say which capabilities have stopped and that nothing is required from them, with no reconnection step attached - because there is no step that would work. Prompting a re-auth against a provider outage produces a person who tries three times and then opens a ticket
  - -> x.informed (node)
- **a.partial** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Name exactly which capabilities stopped, which are still running, and the single re-authorisation step. Telling somebody their whole connection is broken when most of it works buys an afternoon of unnecessary work and a lasting distrust of the next notice
  - -> w.revalidate (node)
- **a.total** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say the connection is carrying nothing at present, name what has stopped depending on it, and give the single re-authorisation step. What stopped is the part the holder needs in order to judge how urgent this is
  - -> w.revalidate (node)
- **x.informed** [exit]
  - informed, recovery not the holder's to perform
  - Detail: if the cause is later reclassified as holder-fixable, this qualifies again on the re-authorisation path
- **w.revalidate** [wait]
  - until the connection revalidates against a new authorization, or the holder removes the connection, or the failure is reclassified as not the holder's to fix
  - Detail: timeout after The re-authorisation step is left open for a bounded response window from the notice; a connection still broken past it is judged against independent relationship evidence, never labelled from this break alone. (configure integration_reconnect.revalidate)
  - a broken connection nobody has reconnected is a churn signal long before it is reported as one, and it must be handed over while that is still true
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.outcome [on event] (node)
  - -> a.unreconnected [on timeout] (node)
- **c.outcome** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What ended the wait?
  - -> a.restored [Reconnected]: revalidation confirms the capabilities are working against the new authorization (node)
  - -> x.removed [Removed]: the holder deliberately disconnected rather than re-authorising (node)
  - -> a.inform-only [Not theirs after all]: the cause was reclassified and no holder action would have helped (node, back-edge)
- **a.unreconnected** [action]
  - Record that the recovery window closed with the connection still broken, as a dated fact about this integration rather than a conclusion about the relationship. A connection somebody chose not to restore is one signal, and it may be the deliberate end of a use case rather than a customer leaving
  - -> c.relationship-signal (node)
- **a.restored** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Confirm which capabilities are working again and name any that came back reduced. A reconnection that silently restores less than before is discovered later, at whatever moment the missing capability was needed
  - -> x.restored (node)
- **x.removed** [exit] (terminal)
  - connection deliberately removed by the holder
  - Detail: connecting the same provider again is a new connection and enters through activation, not through this journey
- **c.relationship-signal** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is there independent evidence that the relationship itself is at risk?
  - -> h.abandoned [Corroborated]: risk evidence from another source already exists against this relationship, so the unreconnected integration adds to a picture rather than being the whole of it (node)
  - -> x.unreconnected [This break is the only signal]: nothing else indicates the relationship is at risk (node)
- **x.restored** [exit]
  - reconnected and confirmed
  - Detail: a later break on the same connection is a new instance
- **h.abandoned** [handoff]
  - Churn risk escalation → evidence → intervention priority
  - Detail: a connection the holder could re-authorise and has not, past the point where the break is still being treated as an interruption
  - carries: which capabilities have been unavailable and for how long
  - carries: what was already said about the break and on which routes
  - carries: that this is one contributing signal alongside evidence that already existed, not a churn conclusion drawn from a broken connection
  - -> RET-24 (journey)
- **x.unreconnected** [exit]
  - recovery window closed, connection still broken, relationship not labelled at risk
  - Detail: a further disconnection event, or a re-authorisation attempt, opens recovery again
