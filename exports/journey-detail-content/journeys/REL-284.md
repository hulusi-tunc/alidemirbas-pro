## REL-284 — Invitation Reminder

- Canonical ID: `REL-284`
- Slug: `relationship-invitation`

- Canonical name (chain form): Relationship invitation → counterparty acceptance → active or expired
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/relationship-invitation`

- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Put a proposed link in front of the party who has to accept it, on terms they can see before they answer, and close the question one way or the other before the invitation goes stale.

> Put a proposed link in front of the party who has to accept it, on terms they can see before they answer, and close the question one way or the other before the invitation goes stale.

### Trigger
- Event: `relationship_invitation_issued`
- Meaning: an invitation recorded by a party holding the authority to extend it; a named counterparty, the scope of the proposed link and its direction; an expiry on the invitation
- Not enough on its own: a shared attribute between two records - an address, a name, a device; an intention to invite that has not been recorded as an invitation
- Requires: an invitation recorded by a party holding the authority to extend it; a named counterparty, the scope of the proposed link and its direction; an expiry on the invitation
- Source: authoritative

### Entity

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] An invitation is not a relationship. Nothing is linked, and nothing is shown to either side as linked, until the counterparty accepts.
- [canonical rule] Acceptance links two records and merges none. Both parties keep their own identity, history and lifecycle.
- [canonical rule] Scope and direction are stated in the invitation itself, not discovered after acceptance.
- [canonical rule] One reminder, never two. The expiry is the pressure; repetition is not.
- [canonical rule] Every invitation expires. A pending claim on somebody who never agreed to it does not sit open indefinitely.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] link active, both identities intact (re-entry: a further invitation between the same parties, for a different scope, is a new instance)
- [failure] invitation declined or withdrawn (re-entry: the inviting party may issue a fresh invitation, which is a new instance; a declined one is never revived)
- [timeout] expired unanswered (re-entry: a fresh invitation from the same party starts a new window)

### Reusable rule
An invitation is a question put to somebody who owes no answer, so it names its scope, names its expiry, and asks at most once more.

### Entity (notes)
- the invitation, plus the two parties and the scope of the link it proposes, in the direction it was issued
- The invitation is the subject, not the parties. A second invitation between the same two parties, for a different scope or the other direction, is its own instance.

### Distinct from
- REL-91 (Entity relationship created → validate → activate or reject) — REL-91 decides whether a link has an authoritative basis and records it active or rejected. This journey exists only in the case where the missing basis is the counterparty's own acceptance, and it establishes nothing itself - it asks.
- SUB-161 (Continuing relationship created → validate → activate or pending) — SUB-161 owns a continuing agreement with a term, effective dates and renewal. Acceptance here creates a link and no term, and an agreement later attached to that link is not this.

### Guardrails

3. Scope and direction are stated in the invitation itself, not discovered after acceptance.
4. One reminder, never two. The expiry is the pressure; repetition is not.
