**Purpose**: Establish whether something delivered has left an obligation unresolved, and which recovery mechanism could satisfy it.

> Establish whether something delivered has left an obligation unresolved, and which recovery mechanism could satisfy it.

### Trigger
- Event: `post_completion_issue_reported`
- Meaning: a concrete problem with a completed fulfillment or service: a wrong item or result, damaged output, a missing component, a quality problem, a service defect, an incorrect configuration or an incomplete outcome
- Not enough on its own: negative feedback about the experience, which reports a feeling rather than naming a problem with what was delivered; a low satisfaction score
- Requires: a concrete problem with a completed fulfillment or service: a wrong item or result, damaged output, a missing component, a quality problem, a service defect, an incorrect configuration or an incomplete outcome
- Source: declared

### Who enters

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] An issue reported is not a confirmed defect.
- [canonical rule] Negative feedback alone does not establish remedy eligibility.
- [canonical rule] Not every issue defaults to a refund.
- [canonical rule] An existing case covering the same obligation suppresses a second recovery lifecycle.

### Recommended orchestration · single-notice
1. **acknowledge** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Does an existing recovery case already cover this problem? · Is there an actionable unresolved obligation?
   - Channel roles: persistent
   - Purpose: Acknowledge and explain, closing according to policy.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [suppression] attached to the existing recovery case (re-entry: if that case closes with the problem still present, the recurrence is assessed on its own terms rather than as a fresh report)
- [no-action] heard; no unresolved obligation and no remedy owed (re-entry: new evidence of an actual defect re-opens this. Repetition of the same report is itself worth reading, without becoming a defect by repetition)
- [handoff] Remedy Confirmation — a confirmed unresolved obligation needing a remedy decision

- `post_completion.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per the completed fulfillment or service; a later instance concerns a different the completed fulfillment or service and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
Post-completion problems should first establish the unresolved obligation before selecting the remedy intended to satisfy it.

### Entity (notes)
- the completed fulfillment or service, and the specific problem reported against it
- The issue is scoped to the fulfillment it concerns. A second problem with the same order is a second issue unless it is the same defect described again.

### Distinct from
- FBK-43 (Feedback received → classify → route → close the loop) — FBK-43 starts from someone's account of an experience and asks whether any operational issue exists. This starts from a concrete problem with something already delivered and asks which recovery route would fix it - the obligation is known to exist and the question is what satisfies it.

### Guardrails
1. An issue reported is not a confirmed defect.
2. Negative feedback alone does not establish remedy eligibility.
3. Not every issue defaults to a refund.
4. An existing case covering the same obligation suppresses a second recovery lifecycle.

### Technical logic (graph, 11 nodes)
- **t.reported** [trigger] (entry, evidence: declared)
  - Event id: `post_completion_issue_reported`
  - Post completion issue reported
  - evidence: declared
  - requires: a concrete problem with a completed fulfillment or service: a wrong item or result, damaged output, a missing component, a quality problem, a service defect, an incorrect configuration or an incomplete outcome
  - not enough on its own: negative feedback about the experience, which reports a feeling rather than naming a problem with what was delivered
  - not enough on its own: a low satisfaction score
  - -> a.capture (node)
- **a.capture** [action]
  - Capture the issue id, the fulfillment or service it concerns, the problem as reported, the affected scope, when it was reported and whatever evidence exists
  - writes issue_log (append)
  - -> c.duplicate (node)
- **c.duplicate** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does an existing recovery case already cover this problem?
  - -> a.attach [Already covered]: an open case concerns the same defect on the same obligation (node)
  - -> a.assess [Nothing open]: no existing case covers it (node)
- **a.attach** [action]
  - Attach the new evidence and context to the existing case. No second recovery lifecycle is opened - two remedies running against one obligation produce two replacements or two refunds, and the second is found by accounting rather than by the process that issued it
  - writes issue_log (append)
  - -> x.attached (node)
- **a.assess** [action]
  - Establish whether the report describes an unresolved obligation a remedy could satisfy, or an experience that fell short without anything having gone wrong. Both are real; only the first creates something to fix
  - -> c.actionable (node)
- **x.attached** [exit]
  - attached to the existing recovery case
  - Detail: if that case closes with the problem still present, the recurrence is assessed on its own terms rather than as a fresh report
- **c.actionable** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is there an actionable unresolved obligation?
  - -> a.classify [Actionable]: something identifiable was owed and is not what was delivered (node)
  - -> a.acknowledge [Not actionable]: the delivery matched what was owed and the experience still disappointed (node)
- **a.classify** [action]
  - Classify the remedy route the problem actually implies - a correction, a reperformance, a replacement, a return, a refund review, a service recovery, or another policy-defined remedy. Refund is one route among several rather than the default, and choosing it because it is the easiest to execute leaves the customer without the thing they wanted
  - writes issue_log (append)
  - -> h.remedy (node)
- **a.acknowledge** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Acknowledge and explain, closing according to policy. No defect is manufactured to give the report somewhere to go, and no refund is issued as a way of ending the conversation
  - writes issue_log (append)
  - -> x.no-defect (node)
- **h.remedy** [handoff]
  - Remedy selection → resolve obligation → financial handoff if needed
  - Detail: a confirmed unresolved obligation needing a remedy decision
  - carries: the unresolved obligation, stated as what is owed rather than as what was complained about
  - carries: the routes the classification suggests, and the evidence behind them
  - -> REM-157 (journey)
- **x.no-defect** [exit]
  - heard; no unresolved obligation and no remedy owed
  - Detail: new evidence of an actual defect re-opens this. Repetition of the same report is itself worth reading, without becoming a defect by repetition
