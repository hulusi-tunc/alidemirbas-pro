## DEC-267 — Adverse Decision Recovery

- Canonical ID: `DEC-267`
- Slug: `adverse-decision-remediation`
- Display name: Adverse Decision Recovery
- Canonical name (chain form): Adverse decision → stated remediation path → corrected or final
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/adverse-decision-remediation`
- Category: Approvals, decisions, reviews & human-in-the-loop work
- Goal: decision-approval
- Channels: email, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Tell somebody a decision went against them and which of exactly three things is true - correctable now, reapplicable later, or final - so the outcome arrives as a position they can act on instead of a verdict they have to interpret.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] adverse decision remediated within its window (re-entry: a further decision against the same subject is a new instance)
- [invalid-state] decision withdrawn before remediation (re-entry: if the decision is reinstated, it enters again as a new adverse decision)
- [failure] decided against, with a stated route to a fresh request (re-entry: a request made under those conditions is a new decision, not a continuation of this one)
- [failure] final; no path onward is defined (re-entry: a change in the governing policy or in the underlying facts is a new decision case, not a reopening of this one)

### Reusable rule
An adverse decision that does not name its own route back, or the honest absence of one, is a verdict rather than an outcome.

### Entity (notes)
- the adverse decision and the subject it was issued against
- The decision is the subject. A second adverse decision on the same person is its own instance with its own path, however similar the reason looks.

### Distinct from
- DEC-186 (Rejection decision → record reason → remediate, close or reapply) — DEC-186 records the rejection, its reason category, and the remediation or reapplication semantics policy defines. This says them out loud to the requester and holds the window open while they act.
- RSK-194 (Policy violation detected → validate → correct, restrict or escalate) — RSK-194 establishes that a rule was broken against the version that governed it. This starts only once that is confirmed and never re-argues whether it happened.

### Guardrails
1. Nothing is said before the decision is authoritatively recorded. A warning about an outcome nobody has reached is how a support queue fills with cases that do not exist.
2. Correction, reapplication and appeal rights are stated only where policy defines them. None is ever invented to make the message land more softly.
3. The decision itself is never re-argued here. Whether it was correct belongs upstream.
4. Where the path is final, no call to action is attached.
5. A resolved decision is confirmed explicitly - silence is read as it still standing.

- **a.final** [action] (execution: communication)

- **c.corrected** [condition] (3 branches)
