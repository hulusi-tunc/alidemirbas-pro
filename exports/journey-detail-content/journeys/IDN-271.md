## IDN-271 — Account Security Alert

- Canonical ID: `IDN-271`
- Slug: `compromise-alert-verification`
- Display name: Account Security Alert
- Canonical name (chain form): Compromise signal → scoped containment → owner verification → recover or clear
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/compromise-alert-verification`
- Category: Identity, verification, authentication & account integrity
- Goal: escalation-exception
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Ask the owner the one question that resolves a suspected compromise, on a route the suspicion does not touch, while saying plainly whether anything has actually been restricted.

> Tell the account owner, at every verified destination the signal does not implicate, what was seen and what was restricted - and let their answer resolve it: cleared, or into secure recovery.

### Trigger
- Event: `compromise_incident_opened_with_scope_determined`
- Meaning: a security incident authoritatively opened against the account; the affected scope determined - which sessions, credentials and capabilities; a record of whether containment was applied and how wide
- Not enough on its own: a single failed authentication; a risk score with no incident behind it; an unusual location with no other signal
- Requires: a security incident authoritatively opened against the account; the affected scope determined - which sessions, credentials and capabilities; a record of whether containment was applied and how wide
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the account plus this security incident and the scope it affects
- instance: account_id + incident_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a compromise incident is opened for the account with its scope determined
- at least one verified destination exists that the signal does not implicate
- the incident has a review point set

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] No alert goes to a destination the signal implicates; where every route is implicated the alert is withheld and the incident proceeds on the security side alone.
- [canonical rule] Suspected is not confirmed: the alert says what was restricted only where something was, and says plainly that nothing was restricted where nothing was.
- [canonical rule] The alert goes to every verified, unimplicated destination at once; a single destination that may itself be compromised is never the only one told.
- [canonical rule] An incident open past its review point is stated to the owner as open, with what remains restricted; it is not silently left in place.
- [canonical rule] Security alerts are exempt from pressure caps and marketing permission; only hard gates (GLB-31) apply, and a fraud hold does not stop an alert to the owner at an unimplicated destination.

**Channel roles** (canonical rule)
- persistent (email) — a verified address the signal does not implicate exists; the alert must be kept and re-read
- urgent (sms) — a verified number the signal does not implicate exists; the shortest route to the confirmation question
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [no-action] no destination outside the suspicion; no alert sent (re-entry: if a destination outside the affected scope is established, the verification question runs from there)
- [success] cleared; restrictions lifted and the owner told (re-entry: a further signal on the same account is a new incident and a new instance)
- [timeout] incident open past its review point; the owner knows where it sits (re-entry: the conclusion, whenever it lands, re-enters on the cleared or the recovery path)
- [handoff] Account Recovery Verification — a confirmed compromise where the owner needs secure control of the account rebuilt

### Reusable rule
A security question can only be asked on a route the suspicion does not reach, and it has to be as easy to answer no as yes.

### Entity (notes)
- the account plus this security incident and the scope it affects
- The incident is the subject and it has its own record. A later signal on the same account is a separate incident and gets its own question.

### Distinct from
- IDN-90 (Suspected account compromise → contain → verify → recover or clear) — IDN-90 determines scope, applies reversible containment and concludes the investigation. This journey does none of that; it is the owner's side of the same incident and it starts only once the scope is known.
- IDN-88 (Account recovery request → prove control → restore secure access) — IDN-88 restores control to somebody who cannot get in. Here the owner still has access, and the open question is whether somebody else does too.

### Guardrails
1. The alert goes only to destinations outside the affected scope. A notice delivered to the compromised route warns the wrong person.
2. Suspected is not confirmed, and the message says which one this is.
3. Where nothing was restricted, the message says so. Implying a lockout that did not happen is its own harm.
4. The all-clear is sent as deliberately as the alert was, because most signals in this category clear.
5. A cleared signal stays in the security history; erasing it makes a recurrence look like a first occurrence.

- **c.route** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is there a destination the signal does not implicate?

  - -> x.withheld [Every route implicated]: the only destinations were changed in the suspicious window or sit behind the same access under question (node)
- **c.contained** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Has anything actually been restricted?
  - -> a.alert-contained [Containment applied]: sessions, credentials or capabilities were restricted, so the owner will meet the restriction whether or not we say so first (node)
  - -> a.alert-watch [Nothing restricted]: the evidence did not justify restriction and the account is operating normally (node)
- **x.withheld** [exit]
  - no destination outside the suspicion; no alert sent

- **a.alert-contained** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say what was restricted, why, and that confirming whether the recent activity was theirs is what resolves it. A restriction somebody walks into unwarned is indistinguishable from the compromise it was meant to contain

- **a.alert-watch** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Ask whether the recent activity was theirs and say plainly that nothing has been restricted. Suspected is not confirmed, and most of these signals are not - a message that implies otherwise converts a false positive into a frightened person
  - -> w.verify (node)
- **w.verify** [wait]
  - until the owner confirms the activity was theirs, or the owner reports the activity was not theirs, or the security review concludes

  - an open suspicion with no stated conclusion is a restriction with no end date, and the owner is the one living inside it
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.outcome [on event] (node)
  - -> a.standing [on timeout] (node)
- **c.outcome** [condition] (2 branches)
  - What did the answer establish?

  - -> h.recovery [Not theirs]: the owner reports the activity was not theirs, or the review confirms the compromise (node)
- **a.standing** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the owner the incident is still open past its review point, what remains restricted, and that nothing further is required from them. A restriction with no stated owner and no stated date is where duplicate cases come from
  - -> x.open (node)
- **a.cleared** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say the signal is cleared and name anything that has been lifted, so a restriction met yesterday is not still assumed today. The record of the signal stays; only the restriction goes

- **h.recovery** [handoff]
