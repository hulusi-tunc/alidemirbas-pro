## CON-264 — Contact Verification

- Canonical ID: `CON-264`
- Slug: `contact-point-confirmation`
- Display name: Contact Verification
- Canonical name (chain form): Contact point added or changed → confirm → permitted or lapsed
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/contact-point-confirmation`
- Category: Consent, preferences, communication & contactability
- Goal: consent-permission
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Establish that the person who owns a new destination actually asked for it, before anything is ever sent there - and tell the destination it replaces, because a change nobody made is only visible from the address it is being taken away from.

> Make a new or changed contact point usable only when the destination itself confirms it - telling the destination being replaced that it is being replaced - and leave it unusable if it never does.

### Trigger
- Event: `contact_point_added_or_changed`
- Meaning: a new or changed contact point recorded against a known identity; a destination in a form that can actually be sent to
- Not enough on its own: a destination typed into a form and not yet submitted; a live session on the account, which proves control of the account rather than of the destination
- Requires: a new or changed contact point recorded against a known identity; a destination in a form that can actually be sent to
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the specific contact point being added or changed, plus the identity it is claimed for
- instance: contact_point_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a contact point is added or changed for an identity
- the new destination is well-formed and deliverable in principle
- no verification instance is already open for this contact point
- change_source is not CON-272's own repair flow - a destination CON-272 already confirmed at its own a.confirm is not asked to confirm itself a second time for the same change

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] The confirmation request goes to the new destination itself and nowhere else; a confirmation answered from inside an authenticated session proves control of the session, not of the destination.
- [canonical rule] A destination being replaced is told it is being replaced, with what it is being replaced by and how to stop it; a silent replacement is how an account is taken over.
- [canonical rule] One reminder at the same destination, naming the point after which nothing will be sent there; then nothing more.
- [canonical rule] Permission is held by the destination that confirmed and by that destination only; an unconfirmed destination stays unusable for anything.
- [canonical rule] Verification messages are exempt from pressure caps and marketing permission; only hard gates (GLB-31) apply.

**Channel roles** (canonical rule)
- persistent (email) — the destination being confirmed or replaced is an address - the message goes to that destination itself
- urgent (sms) — the destination being confirmed or replaced is a number - the message goes to that destination itself
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] confirmed and permitted for this destination (re-entry: a later change to the same contact point is a new instance with its own confirmation)
- [timeout] unconfirmed; the destination stays unusable (re-entry: the same destination submitted again starts a fresh confirmation, not a continuation of this one)
- [invalid-state] superseded before confirmation (re-entry: the destination that replaced it runs its own confirmation)
- [handoff] Identity Attribute Update — a contact point change the previous destination says it did not make

### Configure
- `contact_verification.confirmation_window` [config required] (response-window): configure contact_verification.confirmation_window — The confirmation window is the one defined for this kind of destination; the reminder is placed when it passes unanswered, and the remainder of the window bounds the reminder.
- `contact_verification.window_remainder` [recommended default] (attribute-bound): the remainder of the confirmation window, counted from the first request — After the reminder, only the remainder of the original confirmation window is waited; the window is never extended by the reminder.
- `contact_verification.reminders` [recommended default]: 1 — Only the reminder is discretionary; the confirmation request and the notice to the replaced destination are the verification itself.
- `contact_verification.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — Verification is per contact point change; a further change is a new instance and supersedes the open one.

### Reusable rule
A destination is not yours to send to until the person on the other end of it has said so from that end.

### Entity (notes)
- the specific contact point being added or changed, plus the identity it is claimed for
- One destination, one confirmation. A second contact point on the same identity is its own instance and confirms on its own terms.

### Distinct from
- CON-31 (Permission capture → validate scope → activate or reject) — CON-31 records what was authorised, for which purpose and scope. This runs earlier and answers a different question - whether the destination belongs to the person the permission would be recorded against.
- IDN-89 (Identity attribute change → verify if required → update → propagate) — IDN-89 changes the attribute and reconciles what depended on it. This is the exchange with the two destinations that decides whether the new one is usable at all.

### Guardrails
1. Nothing is sent to an unconfirmed destination except the request to confirm it.
2. The confirmation goes to the destination in question; the change alert goes to the one it replaces. Neither substitutes for the other.
3. Permission never transfers from a replaced destination to its replacement.
4. Unconfirmed and refused are recorded as different facts - one is silence, the other is a decision.

- **a.confirm-new** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Ask the new destination itself to confirm, and send that request nowhere else. A confirmation answered from inside the account proves control of the account, which was never the thing in doubt

- **w.confirm** [wait]
  - until the new destination confirms from inside itself, or the old destination disputes the change, or the contact point is withdrawn or changed again before confirmation

  - -> a.remind [on timeout] (node)
- **c.resolution** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What ended the wait?

- **a.activate** [action]
