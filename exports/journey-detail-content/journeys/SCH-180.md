## SCH-180 — Booking Reschedule

- Canonical ID: `SCH-180`
- Slug: `provider-cancellation`
- Display name: Booking Reschedule
- Canonical name (chain form): Provider or resource cancellation → reallocate → reschedule, remedy or cancel
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/provider-cancellation`
- Category: Scheduling, appointments, reservations & time-bound commitments
- Goal: recovery-retry
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Recover a commitment we cannot keep, without any of the cost landing on the person who was ready.

> Recover a commitment we cannot keep, without any of the cost landing on the person who was ready.

### Trigger
- Event: `provider_or_resource_cannot_fulfil`
- Meaning: a confirmed reservation that can no longer be fulfilled by its assigned provider or resource - unavailability, resource failure, location closure, withdrawn capacity or an operational incident
- Not enough on its own: a customer asking to move the booking, which is their reschedule and not our failure; a provider's tentative doubt that has not become an inability
- Requires: a confirmed reservation that can no longer be fulfilled by its assigned provider or resource - unavailability, resource failure, location closure, withdrawn capacity or an operational incident
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the affected reservations and the provider or resource failure behind them
- instance: booking_id + provider_failure_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- a confirmed reservation that can no longer be fulfilled by its assigned provider or resource - unavailability, resource failure, location closure, withdrawn capacity or an operational incident
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the affected reservations and the provider or resource failure behind them
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] A provider cancellation is not a customer cancellation.
- [canonical rule] An affected customer is never classified as a no-show.
- [canonical rule] A replacement must satisfy the service's actual requirements rather than merely being available.
- [canonical rule] Financial and remedy consequences remain separate lifecycle decisions.

### Recommended orchestration · notice-then-confirm
1. **inform** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is an equivalent replacement available for the same time? · Does the reallocation change anything the customer needs to know?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Tell them what changed, distinguished from a reschedule because the time did not move.
2. **notify-provider-cancel** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is an equivalent replacement available for the same time? · Do the applicable decision rules permit rescheduling this booking?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Tell the customer the confirmed commitment can no longer be kept, that the failure is ours and not theirs, and that they are not recorded as having cancelled or missed it.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- urgent (sms) — an asserted time bound lies inside the urgent horizon and permission for messages on this channel is recorded
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] reallocated; the time and the commitment both stand (re-entry: the booking continues to its scheduled occurrence and revalidates there like any other)
- [failure] cancelled on the provider side; recorded as ours, with nothing attributed to the customer (re-entry: a new booking is a new commitment. This cancellation stays in the record as a provider failure, which is what any later question about the customer's booking history depends on)
- [handoff] Reschedule Validation — a provider-side failure that can be recovered at another time
- [handoff] Remedy Confirmation — a service obligation left unresolved by a provider-side failure
- [handoff] Refund Request — a provider-cancelled booking that was paid for

### Configure
- `provider_cancellation.touches` [recommended default]: 2 — Every touch runs against a budget fixed when the instance opened; the budget is the plan's own length, and no touch is repeated because nothing could tell whether it arrived.
- `provider_cancellation.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per the affected reservations and the provider or resource failure behind them; a later instance concerns a different the affected reservations and the provider or resource failure behind them and no cooldown applies between them.

- guardrails: complaint · message_after_success · unsubscribe

### Reusable rule
Provider-side inability should preserve the customer's underlying service obligation and attempt recovery before treating the reservation as simply cancelled.

### Entity (notes)
- the affected reservations and the provider or resource failure behind them
- The scope is every booking the failure touches. A closed location is not one cancellation, and treating it as one leaves the rest to be discovered by the people who turn up.

### Distinct from
- FUL-145 (Fulfillment exception → diagnose → recover, substitute or fail) — FUL-145 handles a generic fulfillment exception. This carries the scheduling-specific recovery: the same time with a different provider, a different time with the same commitment, and the rule that the customer is never recorded as the cause.

### Guardrails
1. A provider cancellation is not a customer cancellation.
2. An affected customer is never classified as a no-show.
3. A replacement must satisfy the service's actual requirements rather than merely being available.
4. Financial and remedy consequences remain separate lifecycle decisions.

### Technical logic (graph, 18 nodes)
- **t.cannot** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `provider_or_resource_cannot_fulfil`
  - Provider or resource cannot fulfil
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: a confirmed reservation that can no longer be fulfilled by its assigned provider or resource - unavailability, resource failure, location closure, withdrawn capacity or an operational incident
  - not enough on its own: a customer asking to move the booking, which is their reschedule and not our failure
  - not enough on its own: a provider's tentative doubt that has not become an inability
  - -> a.scope (node)
- **a.scope** [action]
  - Identify every reservation the failure affects, and its scope. A closed location is not one cancellation - treating it as one produces a correct outcome for the booking that raised the alarm and silence for the forty behind it, each of whom finds out at the door
  - writes occurrence_log (append)
  - -> a.protect (node)
- **a.protect** [action]
  - Record that the failure is provider-side. Whatever follows, the customer is not marked as having cancelled and is never classified as a no-show - they were available and the service was not, and the record has to say so before anything else touches this booking
  - writes occurrence_log (append)
  - -> c.replacement (node)
- **c.replacement** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is an equivalent replacement available for the same time?
  - -> a.reallocate [Available and permitted]: another provider or resource can deliver the booked service at the booked time, and the rules allow substituting (node)
  - -> c.reschedule [Not at this time]: nothing equivalent is free, or substitution is not permitted for this service (node)
- **a.reallocate** [action]
  - Reallocate to the replacement, preserving the commitment and the time. The replacement has to actually satisfy the service's requirements - a different provider who cannot perform the booked service is not a replacement, and substituting one moves the failure from before the appointment to during it
  - writes reservation_log (append)
  - -> a.release-old (node)
- **c.reschedule** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Do the applicable decision rules permit rescheduling this booking?
  - -> h.reschedule [Permitted]: the service can be delivered at another time and the rules allow moving it (node)
  - -> a.cancel [Not permitted]: the service was time-specific, or the rules do not allow it to move (node)
- **a.release-old** [action]
  - Release the obsolete allocation, idempotently. The original resource returns to availability once, whatever number of times the release is attempted
  - writes reservation_log (append)
  - -> c.notify (node)
- **h.reschedule** [handoff]
  - Reschedule request → recheck availability → move, reject or preserve original
  - Detail: a provider-side failure that can be recovered at another time
  - carries: the original commitment and the requirements a replacement slot has to satisfy
  - carries: the explicit fact that this move originates on our side, which changes what the customer may be offered and what they may be charged
  - -> SCH-175 (journey)
- **a.cancel** [action]
  - Cancel the affected reservation, recorded as provider-side. Provider cancellation and customer cancellation are different terminal states with different consequences, and collapsing them charges a cancellation fee to someone whose appointment we could not keep
  - writes reservation_log (append)
  - -> a.release-cancel (node)
- **c.notify** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the reallocation change anything the customer needs to know?
  - -> a.inform [It does]: the provider, the location or the preparation is different (node)
  - -> x.reallocated [It does not]: nothing visible to the customer changed (node)
- **a.release-cancel** [action]
  - Release the allocation and stop the obsolete preparation, reminders and check-in actions. A reminder for an appointment we cancelled brings the customer in for a service that has no provider waiting
  - writes reservation_log (append)
  - writes suppressed_sends (append)
  - -> a.notify-provider-cancel (node)
- **a.inform** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell them what changed, distinguished from a reschedule because the time did not move. Someone who reads 'your appointment has changed' and assumes the time moved will miss an appointment we successfully saved
  - writes occurrence_log (append)
  - -> x.reallocated (node)
- **x.reallocated** [exit]
  - reallocated; the time and the commitment both stand
  - Detail: the booking continues to its scheduled occurrence and revalidates there like any other
- **a.notify-provider-cancel** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the customer the confirmed commitment can no longer be kept, that the failure is ours and not theirs, and that they are not recorded as having cancelled or missed it. Sent before any remedy or refund is worked out - waiting for the consequence means the person finds out their booking is gone from a message about money
  - -> c.remedy (node)
- **c.remedy** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What does the failure leave to answer for?
  - -> h.remedy [An unresolved service obligation]: the customer still needs the service and it has not been delivered (node)
  - -> h.financial [Money to return]: the booking was paid for or a fee was taken (node)
  - -> x.cancelled-provider [Neither]: nothing was owed and nothing was paid (node)
- **h.remedy** [handoff]
  - Remedy selection → resolve obligation → financial handoff if needed
  - Detail: a service obligation left unresolved by a provider-side failure
  - carries: the obligation as it stands and the fact that the customer did nothing wrong - provider_failure_id stands in for REM-157's obligation_id
  - carries: the explicit fact that the impact of the failure is a separate question from the obligation, and compensation is decided on its own terms
  - carries: a fresh issue_id, minted at this handoff and deterministically derived from provider_failure_id - SCH-180 has no issue concept of its own, so REM-157's instance is opened here rather than carried
  - -> REM-157 (journey)
- **h.financial** [handoff]
  - Refund request → eligibility → approve, reject or review
  - Detail: a provider-cancelled booking that was paid for
  - carries: what was paid and the fact that the cancellation was ours
  - carries: the explicit fact that no cancellation fee applies to a booking the provider could not keep
  - carries: a fresh refund_request_id, minted at this handoff and deterministically derived from provider_failure_id
  - -> FIN-137 (journey)
- **x.cancelled-provider** [exit]
  - cancelled on the provider side; recorded as ours, with nothing attributed to the customer
  - Detail: a new booking is a new commitment. This cancellation stays in the record as a provider failure, which is what any later question about the customer's booking history depends on
