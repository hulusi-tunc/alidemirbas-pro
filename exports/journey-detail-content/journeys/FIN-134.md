## FIN-134 — Payment Failure Recovery

- Canonical ID: `FIN-134`
- Slug: `payment-failure-recovery`
- Display name: Payment Failure Recovery
- Canonical name (chain form): Payment failure → classify → recover, alternate or exit
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/payment-failure-recovery`
- Category: Transactions, payments, billing & financial outcomes
- Goal: recovery-retry
- Channels: email, in-app, push, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Respond to the reason a payment actually failed, and keep the obligation alive while doing it.

> Get the obligation paid by responding to the failure that actually happened, while the obligation stays alive and the relationship's own state is decided elsewhere.

### Trigger
- Event: `authoritative_payment_failure`
- Meaning: a payment system stating that the attempt failed, with whatever reason it gave
- Not enough on its own: a timeout, which establishes nothing about whether money moved
- Requires: a payment system stating that the attempt failed, with whatever reason it gave
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the failed attempt, the obligation behind it and the customer relationship it belongs to
- instance: obligation_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- the obligation is still outstanding in the system of record
- the failure class is established from the provider's own response - temporary, customer-fixable, or declined with or without a usable reason - before anything is retried or said
- no recovery instance is already open for this obligation; a second failure on the same obligation is an event inside the open instance (GLB-19)
- the relationship has not been terminated
- hard gates (GLB-31) permit obligation communication to this person - a closed account still receives it, a fraud or security hold does not

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Exit the moment the obligation is satisfied, cancelled or waived by any means. A recovery message about a paid obligation is the failure this journey exists to prevent.
- [canonical rule] No corrective request is sent while a system-side retry is scheduled and may still succeed. The failure class decides: a temporary failure retries silently first.
- [canonical rule] No corrective request is sent for a decline whose reason cannot be turned into an instruction the customer can act on; the alternate-route path applies instead.
- [canonical rule] An attempt whose outcome is unknown is reconciled first (FIN-135, GLB-20). Unknown is not failed, and nothing is sent or retried on it.
- [canonical rule] Hard gates (GLB-31) apply. Pressure caps do not: this is transactional communication about an obligation the person already holds, and it is deduplicated by obligation and touch rather than rationed.

### Recommended orchestration · notice-then-confirm
1. **corrective-request** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: What kind of failure was it?
   - Channel roles: in-session -> persistent
   - Purpose: Name the exact corrective action - update the method, complete the authentication, choose another method - and what is owed. Provider risk detail and internal decline codes are never shown.
   - destination: `payment-method-update` · bound to `obligation_id` · must not claim: the reason the provider gave, beyond its class, that the obligation is waived, reduced or extended
2. **offer-alternate** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: What kind of failure was it? · Is an alternative valid payment route available, and whose decision is it?
   - Channel roles: in-session -> persistent
   - Purpose: Put the available alternative methods in front of the customer and ask which to use, stating that the obligation stands either way. Choosing a payment method is theirs to make.
   - destination: `payment-method-choice` · bound to `obligation_id` · must not claim: that a method was chosen for them
3. **reminder** (transactional, optional strategy)
   - Timing: One reminder, placed so the customer can still act before the consequence date. Where no consequence date exists the reminder point is the recovery window itself and the reminder is skipped. (example: 2 days–3 days; configure payment.reminder_point) (relative to consequence_date)
   - cancelled by the obligation is satisfied in the system of record; the holder stops pursuing the route back
   - re-read before sending: the obligation is still outstanding and the method is still invalid, read from the system of record immediately before the reminder
   - checks: Is a reminder still useful, and is there a consequence to name?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: One reminder naming the consequence and its date, with the same corrective action. It is not repeated.
   - destination: `payment-method-update` · bound to `obligation_id` · must not claim: the reason the provider gave, beyond its class
   - after t1
4. **confirmation** (transactional, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: How did recovery end?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Confirm the obligation is discharged and that nothing further is expected. An obligation met and never acknowledged is one the person keeps checking.
   - destination: `obligation-receipt` · bound to `obligation_id`
   - after t1

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- in-session (in-app) — the person has an active session in the product - the corrective action is a form, and the shortest route to it is inside the product
- persistent (email) — no active session, or the instruction and its link have to survive until the person can act
- urgent (sms, push) — an asserted consequence date exists inside the urgent horizon and permission for service messages on this channel is recorded
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] obligation satisfied through recovery (re-entry: a future failure against a future obligation is its own instance)
- [handoff] Grace Period Management — an unpaid obligation entering a grace period
- [handoff] Overdue State Recalculation — an unpaid obligation becoming overdue
- [handoff] Access Suspension — policy restricting capability while an obligation is unpaid

### Configure
- `payment.alternate_choice_window` [recommended default] (response-window): 3 days–7 days — The customer's choice of method is theirs to make and is waited for. An unanswered choice is not a refusal to pay, and the obligation goes on to its consequence rather than being treated as declined.
- `payment.reminder_point` [recommended default] (reminder-before-attribute): 2 days–3 days — One reminder, placed so the customer can still act before the consequence date. Where no consequence date exists the reminder point is the recovery window itself and the reminder is skipped.
- `payment.recovery_window` [config required] (recovery-window): configure payment.recovery_window — The recovery window is the obligation class's own grace or consequence policy. Its end is the consequence, owned by the grace, overdue or restriction lifecycle - never an extension invented here.
- `payment.discretionary_touches` [recommended default]: 1 — Only discretionary messages count against the cap. The corrective request and the discharge confirmation are obligations, not touches to ration.
- `payment.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — Recovery is per obligation. A later failure against a later obligation is its own instance, and no cooldown applies between obligations.

### Reusable rule
Payment recovery should respond to the actual failure class while preserving the underlying financial obligation until it is resolved.

### Entity (notes)
- the failed attempt, the obligation behind it and the customer relationship it belongs to
- Three things, and the failure touches only the first. The obligation stands and the relationship continues while recovery runs.

### Distinct from
- OPS-124 (Retryable failure → backoff → retry → resolve or exhaust) — OPS-124 is a generic technical retry against a transient fault. This makes a business recovery decision: what to ask the customer for, whether an alternative route exists, and what happens to the obligation if none of it works.

### Guardrails
1. A payment failure is not a subscription cancellation.
2. A decline reason is never invented. What the provider said is what the customer is told, or nothing specific is claimed.
3. Retries are bounded.
4. Sensitive provider and internal risk detail is not exposed.

- **a.retry** [action]

- **a.corrective** [action] (execution: communication)
