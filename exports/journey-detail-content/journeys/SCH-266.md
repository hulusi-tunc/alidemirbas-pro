## SCH-266 — Appointment Reminder

- Canonical ID: `SCH-266`
- Slug: `appointment-readiness-reminder`
- Display name: Appointment Reminder
- Canonical name (chain form): Appointment approaching → prerequisites and revalidation → ready, reminded or at risk
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/appointment-readiness-reminder`
- Category: Scheduling, appointments, reservations & time-bound commitments
- Goal: readiness-revalidation
- Channels: email, sms, in-app
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Get the customer's side of a confirmed commitment done before the commitment arrives, and send the reminder from what the booking is at that moment rather than from what it was when it was made.

> Get the customer's side of a confirmed commitment done before it arrives, and remind them from what the booking is at the moment of sending.

### Trigger
- Event: `confirmed_booking_with_customer_owed_prerequisites`
- Meaning: a confirmed booking with a scheduled time; at least one prerequisite recorded as outstanding and owned by the customer
- Not enough on its own: a request that has not been confirmed; a prerequisite owned by the provider rather than by the customer
- Requires: a confirmed booking with a scheduled time; at least one prerequisite recorded as outstanding and owned by the customer
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the confirmed booking occurrence plus the prerequisites its customer owes against it
- instance: booking_id + occurrence_id · one-active-per-key
- canonical rule: A rescheduled occurrence is the same instance re-timed from the new scheduled time. A cancellation or a material change supersedes it; the replacement booking runs its own readiness from its own confirmation.

### Who enters
- the booking is confirmed in the system of record and its scheduled time is in the future
- the customer is contactable for service messages about this booking
- no reminder has been sent for this occurrence
- hard gates (GLB-31) permit service communication to this person

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] Exit on cancellation or material change. A reminder is never built from a stored copy of the booking: every touch re-reads authoritative state first, which is how somebody who cancelled is not told to turn up.
- [canonical rule] One reminder per occurrence. A second reminder sent because nothing could tell whether the first arrived is what teaches people to stop reading them.
- [canonical rule] Where the confirmation already falls inside the pre-start window, no prerequisite prompt is sent; the journey goes straight to revalidation and the reminder.
- [canonical rule] Deduplicated by booking and occurrence against the confirmation (SCH-277) and any reschedule notice (SCH-180) about the same booking.
- [canonical rule] Hard gates (GLB-31) apply. The service pressure class deduplicates against other service messages about this booking; it does not ration a reminder the person is owed.

### Recommended orchestration · deadline-countdown
1. **prerequisite-prompt** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is there enough time left for a prompt to be worth sending?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: Every outstanding prerequisite, whose it is and the point by which each must be done - in one message rather than one per requirement.
   - destination: `booking-prerequisites` · bound to `booking_id`
2. **at-risk-notice** (service-critical, mandatory, canonical rule)
   - Timing: The same pre-start point: the reminder is sent against the booking as it is at that moment. (example: 24 hours–72 hours; configure scheduling.pre_start_window) (relative to scheduled_at)
   - cancelled by the booking is cancelled, moved or materially changed
   - re-read before sending: the booking re-read from authoritative state before either the at-risk notice or the reminder is built
   - checks: Does the booking still stand as it was just read? · Is anything outstanding that would stop the service happening?
   - Channel roles: urgent -> persistent
   - Purpose: The reminder plus the one thing that will stop this going ahead, with the last point at which it can still be done. The confirmed time is not moved.
   - why this priority: the service cannot be delivered without this prerequisite - not saying so costs the person the appointment
   - destination: `booking-prerequisites` · bound to `booking_id`
3. **reminder** (service, canonical rule)
   - Timing: The same pre-start point: the reminder is sent against the booking as it is at that moment. (example: 24 hours–72 hours; configure scheduling.pre_start_window) (relative to scheduled_at)
   - cancelled by the booking is cancelled, moved or materially changed
   - re-read before sending: the booking re-read from authoritative state before either the at-risk notice or the reminder is built
   - checks: Does the booking still stand as it was just read? · Is anything outstanding that would stop the service happening?
   - Channel roles: urgent -> persistent -> in-session
   - Purpose: The time, the place or joining route, and anything still outstanding that does not block the service.
   - destination: `booking-detail` · bound to `booking_id`

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- urgent (sms) — the scheduled time is within the same day, or a critical prerequisite is outstanding, and permission for service SMS is recorded
- persistent (email) — the message lists things to read and do over time, or no urgent channel is permitted
- in-session (in-app) — the person is in the product
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [invalid-state] superseded; the booking changed before the reminder was due (re-entry: the booking that replaced it runs its own readiness from its own confirmation)
- [handoff] Reservation Readiness — a booking reaching its pre-start point with a critical prerequisite still outstanding
- [handoff] Pre-Service Revalidation — reminded against a revalidated booking; the start window and attendance belong to pre-service revalidation

### Configure
- `scheduling.pre_start_window` [recommended default] (reminder-before-attribute): 24 hours–72 hours — The reminder is built at the pre-start point so it reflects the booking as it is then. The point is set from how long the customer's own preparation takes, not from how long ago the booking was made.
- `scheduling.reminder_touches` [recommended default]: 2 — A prerequisite prompt and one reminder per occurrence. The at-risk notice is an obligation to the person and sits outside the cap.
- `scheduling.reminder_cooldown` [recommended default]: none — Per occurrence. The next occurrence of a recurring commitment is its own instance and no cooldown applies between occurrences.

### Reusable rule
A reminder is only as true as the moment it is built, so it is built at the moment it is sent.

### Entity (notes)
- the confirmed booking occurrence plus the prerequisites its customer owes against it
- One occurrence, one instance. Each occurrence of a recurring commitment revalidates and reminds on its own.

### Distinct from
- SCH-174 (Reservation confirmed → prepare → upcoming or ready) — SCH-174 prepares the conditions and records a reminder having been sent as somebody else's fact. This journey is that somebody, and it never moves the confirmed time.
- SCH-177 (Scheduled time approaches → revalidate → check in, start or exception) — SCH-177 revalidates at the pre-start point in order to start the service. This revalidates at the same point in order to decide whether anything should be sent at all.

### Guardrails
1. Nothing is sent without re-reading the booking at send time. The scheduled job carries the version it was built against, and that version is a claim rather than a fact.
2. A reminder having been sent is never recorded as preparation being complete.
3. Interaction with a reminder is not attendance and is not a prerequisite satisfied.
4. Preparation never moves the confirmed time. A failing condition escalates; it does not reschedule.
5. One reminder per occurrence, and outstanding requirements are named together rather than one message each.
