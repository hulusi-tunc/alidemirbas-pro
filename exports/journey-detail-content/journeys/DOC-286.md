### Reusable rule
A document takes effect on its own date, but it becomes real to its holder only when they are told what it now lets them do.

### Entity (notes)
- the effective document version and the entitlement it confers on its holder
- One effective version, one instance. A renewal or a superseding version taking effect is its own instance and inherits nothing from this one.

### Distinct from
- DOC-216 (Signed document → validate completion → effective or pending condition) — DOC-216 establishes whether and when the document takes effect and revalidates it at that moment. This journey starts only once that record exists, and it decides nothing about effectiveness itself.
- ACC-263 (Entitlement or credential issued → activation window → activated or lapsed unclaimed) — ACC-263 runs a claim window on a granted capability that lapses if unused. Here nothing lapses for want of use - the document is in force either way, and dormancy is an observation rather than a loss.

### Guardrails
1. Signed is not effective, and only the effective record starts this.
2. The entitlement is named as what the holder can do, never as what the document says.
3. An entitlement with nothing to exercise is never chased for use.
4. One reminder, never two. The entitlement stands whether or not anybody takes it up.
5. Dormant and no-longer-in-force are recorded as different outcomes - one is about the holder, the other is about the document.
