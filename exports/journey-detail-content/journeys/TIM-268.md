## TIM-268 — Action Required Reminder

- Canonical ID: `TIM-268`
- Slug: `outstanding-obligation-reminder`
- Display name: Action Required Reminder
- Canonical name (chain form): Customer-owed action → deadline reminder → completed or lapsed
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/outstanding-obligation-reminder`
- Category: Time, deadlines, expiry & temporary states
- Goal: escalation-exception
- Channels: email, sms
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Remind somebody of what they owe while there is still time to do it, from the state the obligation is in at the moment of sending - because a reminder for something already done costs more than the reminder that was never sent.

> Remind somebody of what they owe while there is still time to do it, from the state the obligation is in at the moment of sending - because a reminder for something already done costs more than the reminder that was never sent.

### Trigger
- Event: `customer_owed_obligation_outstanding`
- Meaning: an authoritative record that a defined action is owed by a named person; a due date recorded against it
- Not enough on its own: an internal expectation with no due date; a reminder schedule that fired before the obligation was confirmed outstanding
- Requires: an authoritative record that a defined action is owed by a named person; a due date recorded against it
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the outstanding obligation, its owner and its due date
- instance: obligation_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters

- a due date recorded against it
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the outstanding obligation
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] The record decides what is owed. Nothing sent from here changes it, and no reminder is evidence of it.
- [canonical rule] The obligation is re-read at send time, every time. A reminder for something already done is worse than one that never went.
- [canonical rule] One reminder before the deadline and one notice after it. Repetition past that is a recovery process and belongs elsewhere.
- [canonical rule] Outstanding is not failure. A person who has not yet acted inside their window has done nothing wrong.
- [canonical rule] What is still owed is named, not what was originally owed.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- urgent (sms) — due_at falls inside the urgent_horizon attribute and permission for messages on this channel is recorded
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [no-action] no longer owed; nothing was sent (re-entry: a later obligation owed by the same person is a new instance)
- [success] satisfied and confirmed (re-entry: a new obligation owed by the same person is a new instance)
- [timeout] lapsed unmet; no further consequence is defined (re-entry: if the obligation is later satisfied, the confirmation runs from that event)
- [handoff] external:consequence-owner — an obligation still outstanding past its deadline with a defined consequence attached

- `outstanding_obligation.touches` [recommended default]: 3 — Every touch runs against a budget fixed when the instance opened; the budget is the plan's own length, and no touch is repeated because nothing could tell whether it arrived.

### Reusable rule
A reminder is a claim about the present, so it is built from the present or not sent at all.

### Entity (notes)
- the outstanding obligation, its owner and its due date
- The obligation is the subject and it outlives every reminder about it. Two obligations owed by the same person are two instances.

### Distinct from
- FIN-131 (Financial obligation created → due → satisfied or outstanding) — FIN-131 holds what is owed as its own state, independent of anything sent about it. This is the sending, and it reads that state rather than defining it - nothing here changes what is owed.
- REM-153 (Return authorized → in transit, received, lost or expired) — REM-153 tracks whether the resource actually comes back and holds the states where it might not have. This is the prompt to the person still holding it, and it stops the moment the record says it is no longer outstanding.

### Guardrails
1. The record decides what is owed. Nothing sent from here changes it, and no reminder is evidence of it.
2. The obligation is re-read at send time, every time. A reminder for something already done is worse than one that never went.
3. One reminder before the deadline and one notice after it. Repetition past that is a recovery process and belongs elsewhere.
4. Outstanding is not failure. A person who has not yet acted inside their window has done nothing wrong.
5. What is still owed is named, not what was originally owed.
