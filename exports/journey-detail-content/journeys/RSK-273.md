## RSK-273 — Usage Limit Alert

- Canonical ID: `RSK-273`
- Slug: `usage-limit-capacity-path`
- Display name: Usage Limit Alert
- Canonical name (chain form): Usage limit reached → capacity path → upgrade, wait for reset or stay blocked
- Source route: `/lab/customer-journeys/usage-limit-capacity-path`
- Category: Risk, policy, compliance & exception management
- Goal: access-entitlement-change
- Channels: in-app, email
- Surface: customer (communicating)

**Purpose**: Meet somebody at the moment a limit stops them with the three facts that decide what happens next - what the limit is, when it resets, and whether more capacity can be bought - without any of it reading as an accusation.

> Meet somebody at the moment a limit stops them with the three facts that decide what happens next - what the limit is, when it resets, and whether more capacity can be bought - without any of it reading as an accusation.

### Trigger
- Event: `limit_reached_and_action_blocked`
- Meaning: an authoritative usage figure against a defined limit and measurement window; an action actually blocked or held by that limit
- Not enough on its own: a figure from a lagging or non-authoritative counter; approaching a threshold with nothing yet stopped
- Requires: an authoritative usage figure against a defined limit and measurement window; an action actually blocked or held by that limit
- Source: authoritative

### Entity
- Scope: the entity and the one limit constraining it, in one measurement window
- instance: entity_ref + limit_id + window_id · one-active-per-key

### Who enters
- an authoritative usage figure against a defined limit and measurement window
- an action actually blocked or held by that limit
- no instance of this journey is already open for the the entity and the one limit constraining it
- hard gates (GLB-31) allow communication for this purpose

### Suppressed when
- [canonical rule] A limit reached is not abuse, and the message never borrows the vocabulary of one.
- [canonical rule] A reset point is never invented. If the authoritative source has no date, no date is stated.
- [canonical rule] The free path is named wherever it exists, even in the message that offers the paid one.
- [canonical rule] Where the capacity decision belongs to somebody else, both parties are told - the one waiting and the one who can end the wait.

**Channel roles** (recommended default)
- in-session (in-app) — the person is active in the product and the action is taken there
- persistent (email) — the message has to be kept and survive until the person can act on it
- delivery fallback: same-role-other-channel

### Stops when
- [success] window reset; capacity available again under the same limit (re-entry: reaching the limit again in a later window re-enters here)
- [no-action] at the limit with no route to more capacity in this window (re-entry: a reset, or a change to the limit authorised elsewhere, qualifies this again as a new instance)
- [handoff] Plan Change Validation — an authorised capacity increase that has to be executed as a change of plan or terms

### Configure
- `usage_limit.capacity` [config required] (attribute-bound): configure usage_limit.capacity — The held action waits until the authoritative reset of the window or an authorised capacity increase; no reset point is ever invented.
- `usage_limit.touches` [recommended default]: 2 — The wall notice and the reset notice are mandatory; the discretionary offers run against a budget of the plan's own length.
- `usage_limit.cooldown` [recommended default]: none — This journey is per the entity and the one limit constraining it; a later instance concerns a different the entity and the one limit constraining it and no cooldown applies between them.

### Reusable rule
A limit is only fair at the moment it bites if it can name its own way out, priced or timed.

### Entity (notes)
- the entity and the one limit constraining it, in one measurement window
- One limit, one window, one authoritative count. Reaching it is an operational fact about usage and says nothing about the person.

### Distinct from
- RSK-199 (Limit or quota reached → block, wait, increase or reset) — RSK-199 blocks the action, holds the count and governs the reset. This journey adds nothing operational - it is what the person at the block is told, and it sends only figures RSK-199 already treats as authoritative.
- RSK-193 (Risk threshold crossed → apply scoped restriction → review or release) — RSK-193 restricts in response to risk. A limit reached is the limit working, and routing it through a risk message is how ordinary usage gets treated as suspicion.

### Guardrails
1. A limit reached is not abuse, and the message never borrows the vocabulary of one.
2. A reset point is never invented. If the authoritative source has no date, no date is stated.
3. The free path is named wherever it exists, even in the message that offers the paid one.
4. Where the capacity decision belongs to somebody else, both parties are told - the one waiting and the one who can end the wait.

### Technical logic (graph, 13 nodes)
- **t.blocked** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `limit_reached_and_action_blocked`
  - Limit reached and action blocked
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: an authoritative usage figure against a defined limit and measurement window
  - requires: an action actually blocked or held by that limit
  - not enough on its own: a figure from a lagging or non-authoritative counter
  - not enough on its own: approaching a threshold with nothing yet stopped
  - -> a.at-the-wall (node)
- **a.at-the-wall** [action] (execution: communication)
  - At the point the action is stopped, say which limit was reached, the usage against it, and when the window resets. Reaching a limit is the limit working - anything that reads as an accusation turns an ordinary constraint into a support contact and a grievance
  - -> c.path (node)
- **c.path** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What path exists from here?
  - -> c.decider [Capacity is purchasable]: additional capacity for this limit can be authorised and is a genuine option on this relationship (node)
  - -> w.capacity [Resets on its own]: no more capacity is available in this window, but the reset point is authoritatively defined (node)
  - -> x.blocked [Neither]: the limit is fixed, with no purchasable capacity and no defined reset (node)
- **c.decider** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the person who hit the limit hold the capacity decision?
  - -> a.offer-self [Theirs to decide]: the same party can authorise more capacity (node)
  - -> a.offer-holder [Held elsewhere]: the commercial decision sits with another party on the relationship (node)
- **w.capacity** [wait]
  - until additional capacity is authorised, or the usage limit resets, or the held action is abandoned
  - Detail: timeout after The held action waits until the authoritative reset of the window or an authorised capacity increase; no reset point is ever invented. (configure usage_limit.capacity)
  - the reset is the one date in the message the person is relying on, and passing it in silence makes the message retrospectively false
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.outcome [on event] (node)
  - -> c.outcome [on timeout] (node)
- **x.blocked** [exit]
  - at the limit with no route to more capacity in this window
  - Detail: a reset, or a change to the limit authorised elsewhere, qualifies this again as a new instance
- **a.offer-self** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Name the capacity that would release the held action and what it costs, alongside the reset that would release it for nothing. Withholding the free path in order to sell the paid one is the fastest way to make a limit read as a trap
  - -> w.capacity (node, back-edge)
- **a.offer-holder** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the party who holds the decision what is blocked, for whom, what capacity would release it and what the reset alternative is. This half and the next are separate sends to separate people on separate routes, and either one can fail without the other
  - -> a.notify-blocked-party (node)
- **c.outcome** [condition] (3 branches)
  - What changed?
  - -> h.capacity [Capacity authorised]: more capacity was approved and requires a change of plan or terms to take effect (node)
  - -> a.reset [Window reset]: the authoritative reset occurred and capacity is available again under the same limit (node)
  - -> x.blocked [Still blocked]: neither happened and the action is still held (node, back-edge)
- **a.notify-blocked-party** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Tell the person whose action is held that the decision now sits with somebody else, name who, and give the authoritative point at which the window resets anyway. Told only that they hit a limit, they wait on a person who does not know they are being waited on
  - -> w.capacity (node, back-edge)
- **h.capacity** [handoff]
  - Plan or terms change request → validate → schedule, apply or reject
  - Detail: an authorised capacity increase that has to be executed as a change of plan or terms
  - carries: the limit, the window and the usage that triggered this
  - carries: which action is held and who is waiting on it
  - -> SUB-166 (journey)
- **a.reset** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say the window has reset and the held action can proceed, taken from the authoritative reset rather than an assumed clock. A locally guessed reset grants capacity nobody authorised, and the two drift apart quietly until somebody is refused at a moment we told them they would not be
  - -> x.reset (node)
- **x.reset** [exit]
  - window reset; capacity available again under the same limit
  - Detail: reaching the limit again in a later window re-enters here
