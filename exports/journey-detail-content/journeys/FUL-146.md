**Purpose**: Hold lateness as its own state, with the original commitment intact behind whatever the new estimate is.

### Suppressed when

- [canonical rule] Dates that will not hold are not promised repeatedly. Where no reliable estimate exists, that is what is said.
- [canonical rule] A changed ETA preserves the original commitment history.
- [canonical rule] Communicating a delay does not resolve the operational delay.
- [canonical rule] Once the obligation is handed to a delivery executor, an in-transit slip on the same obligation is FUL-265's delay-or-tracking state to hold and report; this journey does not open a second, competing delay narrative about a slip FUL-265 is already tracking under its own wait.

### Recommended orchestration · offer-decide-remind
1. **delay-update** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is there a reliable new completion estimate? · Does the changed timing alter what the recipient should plan around?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: State the original commitment, the current estimate or the explicit fact that there is not a reliable one, and what is still owed.
2. **no-choice-update** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is there a reliable new completion estimate? · Does the changed timing alter what the recipient should plan around? · Does the delay exceed the acceptable threshold? · Does the counterparty have a decision to make?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Say that the delay is beyond what was committed, that no option is currently available to them, and that it is being escalated rather than left.
3. **offer** (service, canonical rule)
   - sent on classification - no wait before it
   - checks: Is there a reliable new completion estimate? · Does the changed timing alter what the recipient should plan around? · Does the delay exceed the acceptable threshold? · Does the counterparty have a decision to make?
   - Channel roles: persistent -> urgent
   - Purpose: Offer the choices that are actually available - wait, reschedule, an alternative, or cancel.
   - destination: `delay-choices` · bound to `obligation_id` · must not claim: a date that will not hold, a choice that cannot be honoured

### Reusable rule
A fulfillment delay changes the expected timing of an unresolved obligation without pretending the obligation has failed or disappeared.

### Entity (notes)
- the obligation and its timing commitment, with the history of what was promised
- Each revised estimate is appended. Overwriting the original hides a repeated slip, which is the pattern that matters more than any single date.

### Guardrails
1. Delayed is not failed.
2. Dates that will not hold are not promised repeatedly. Where no reliable estimate exists, that is what is said.
3. A changed ETA preserves the original commitment history.
4. Communicating a delay does not resolve the operational delay.

### Technical logic (graph, 19 nodes)
- **t.slip** [trigger] (entry, evidence: authoritative)
  - Event id: `expected_fulfillment_timing_slipped`
  - Expected fulfillment timing slipped
  - evidence: authoritative
  - requires: a material slip against the timing this obligation was committed to
  - not enough on its own: a slip within the tolerance the commitment already allows
  - -> a.assess (node)
- **a.assess** [action]
  - Determine the original commitment, the current estimate, the cause, the affected scope and the impact, and record DELAYED. Delayed is a change to timing and not a failure - the obligation is still owed and nothing downstream may treat it as gone
  - writes fulfillment_log (append)
  - -> c.estimate (node)
- **c.estimate** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Is there a reliable new completion estimate?
  - -> a.update [A reliable estimate]: the cause is understood well enough to predict when it clears (node)
  - -> a.no-estimate [No reliable estimate]: the cause is not understood well enough to name a date that will hold (node)
- **a.update** [action]
  - Update the expected timing, appending to the commitment history. The original commitment is preserved - what was promised and what it became are two facts, and keeping both is the only way a repeated slip becomes visible
  - writes fulfillment_log (append)
  - -> c.recipient-impact (node)
- **a.no-estimate** [action]
  - Record that no reliable estimate exists rather than issuing one. Repeatedly promising dates that do not hold costs more trust than admitting the date is unknown, and each broken date makes the next one worth less
  - writes fulfillment_log (append)
  - -> c.recipient-impact (node)
- **c.recipient-impact** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the changed timing alter what the recipient should plan around?
  - -> a.delay-update [It changes their plans]: the new estimate, or the loss of a reliable one, moves something they arranged their own time or commitments around (node)
  - -> c.threshold [No material change for them]: the slip stays inside what they were already told to expect and nothing they arranged moves (node)
- **a.delay-update** [action] (execution: communication)
  - State the original commitment, the current estimate or the explicit fact that there is not a reliable one, and what is still owed. A slip that is real in the record and invisible to the person waiting is the failure this journey exists to prevent - and it stays true inside tolerance, because tolerance is ours, not theirs
  - -> c.threshold (node)
- **c.threshold** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the delay exceed the acceptable threshold?
  - -> w.resume [Within tolerance]: the new timing is still inside what the commitment or policy accepts (node)
  - -> c.choice [Beyond tolerance]: the delay has passed what the commitment or policy accepts (node)
- **w.resume** [wait]
  - until fulfilment resumes or completes
  - Detail: timeout after The revised horizon. (configure fulfillment_delay.resume)
  - a delay that outlives even its revised horizon has stopped being a timing problem, and communicating about it does not resolve it
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> h.resume [on event] (node)
  - -> h.escalate [on timeout] (node)
- **c.choice** [condition] (2 branches)
  - Does the counterparty have a decision to make?
  - -> a.offer [They choose]: real options exist and the choice between them is theirs (node)
  - -> a.no-choice-update [Nothing to offer]: no option exists that they could meaningfully choose between (node)
- **h.resume** [handoff]
  - Fulfillment execution → progress → complete, partial or fail
  - Detail: a delayed obligation resuming
  - carries: the current commitment and the history of what preceded it
  - carries: the scope already completed, which the delay never touched
  - -> FUL-144 (journey)
- **h.escalate** [handoff]
  - Responsibility escalation → higher authority → resolution or return
  - Detail: a delay beyond tolerance with nothing to offer, or outliving its revised horizon
  - carries: the original commitment, every revision and the cause
  - carries: the fact that communicating about the delay has not resolved the operational problem behind it
  - -> OWN-55 (journey)
- **a.offer** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Offer the choices that are actually available - wait, reschedule, an alternative, or cancel. Offering a choice that cannot be honoured is worse than offering none, because it converts a delay into a broken second promise
  - writes fulfillment_log (append)
  - -> w.decision (node)
- **a.no-choice-update** [action] (execution: communication)
  - Say that the delay is beyond what was committed, that no option is currently available to them, and that it is being escalated rather than left. Escalating in silence tells the recipient nothing is happening at the exact moment most is
  - -> h.escalate (node, back-edge)
- **w.decision** [wait]
  - until the person makes the offered choice
  - Detail: timeout after The decision window. (configure fulfillment_delay.decision)
  - no answer means continue waiting - silence is not consent to cancel something someone is still expecting
  - engagement does not extend the window
  - -> c.decision [on event] (node)
  - -> w.resume [on timeout] (node, back-edge)
- **c.decision** [condition] (4 branches)
  - What did they choose?
  - -> w.resume [Wait]: they accept the revised timing (node, back-edge)
  - -> a.reschedule [Reschedule]: they want a different date or window (node)
  - -> h.exception [An alternative]: they would take something different instead (node)
  - -> h.cancel [Cancel]: they no longer want it (node)
- **a.reschedule** [action]
  - Record the rescheduled commitment, appended to the history rather than replacing what came before it
  - writes fulfillment_log (append)
  - -> w.resume (node, back-edge)
- **h.exception** [handoff]
  - Fulfillment exception → diagnose → recover, substitute or fail
  - Detail: a counterparty choosing an alternative over waiting
  - carries: the alternative they chose
  - carries: the obligation as it currently stands
  - -> FUL-145 (journey)
- **h.cancel** [handoff]
  - Fulfillment cancellation → stop future work → release resources → reconcile
  - Detail: a counterparty cancelling rather than waiting
  - carries: the completed and remaining scope, separately
  - carries: the delay history, which is the reason and belongs in the record
  - -> FUL-150 (journey)
